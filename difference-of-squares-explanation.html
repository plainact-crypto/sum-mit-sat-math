Difference of Squares
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Recognize and factor a difference of two perfect squares.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to spot the pattern quickly, factor it correctly, and check the result by multiplication.

a² − b²→(a − b)(a + b)
02 · CORE IDEA

Two squares. A subtraction sign. Conjugate factors.

A difference of squares has exactly two terms, both terms are perfect squares, and they are separated by subtraction.

Patterna² − b²Difference of squares
Factor(a − b)(a + b)Same terms, opposite signs

The rule works because (a − b)(a + b) = a² + ab − ab − b² = a² − b². The middle terms cancel.

03 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1

Factor: x² − 25

  1. Identify the squares.x² = (x)² and 25 = 5².
  2. Apply the pattern.x² − 5² = (x − 5)(x + 5)
  3. Check.(x − 5)(x + 5) = x² − 25.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2

Factor: 9y² − 16

9y² = (3y)²16 = 4²9y² − 16 = (3y − 4)(3y + 4)

05 · FACTOR COMPLETELY

Factor: 12x² − 75

Always check for a greatest common factor first.

12x² − 75 = 3(4x² − 25)= 3[(2x)² − 5²]= 3(2x − 5)(2x + 5)
Math check(2x − 5)(2x + 5)=4x²−25; multiplying by 3 gives 12x²−75.

06 · COMMON MISTAKES
Using the rule on a sum

x² + 25 is not a difference of squares over the real numbers.

Forgetting square roots of coefficients

For 9x² − 49, use 3x and 7, giving (3x−7)(3x+7).

Stopping before factoring completely

For 8x²−72, first factor 8 to get 8(x²−9), then finish: 8(x−3)(x+3).

07 · EXAM STRATEGY

Run a three-question check.

  1. Is there a GCF to factor first?
  2. Are exactly two terms left with subtraction between them?
  3. Is each term a perfect square?

If all three conditions are satisfied, use (a−b)(a+b) and multiply back to verify.
