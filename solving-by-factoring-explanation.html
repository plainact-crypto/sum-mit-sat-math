Solving by Factoring
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Solve quadratic equations by factoring and using the zero-product property.

Factoring turns one quadratic equation into simpler factor equations. The key is to put the equation in the form quadratic = 0 first.

x² − 5x + 6 = 0→(x−2)(x−3)=0 → x=2 or x=3
02 · CORE METHOD

Zero first, factors second, solutions last.

  1. Move all terms to one side so the other side is 0.
  2. Factor the quadratic completely.
  3. Use the zero-product property: if AB=0, then A=0 or B=0.
  4. Solve each resulting equation.
  5. Substitute each solution into the original equation to verify it.
03 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1

Solve: x² − 7x + 12 = 0

(x−3)(x−4)=0x−3=0 or x−4=0x=3 or x=4

Solve: x² + 2x = 15

x²+2x−15=0(x+5)(x−3)=0x=−5 or x=3

05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — GCF FIRST

Solve: 2x² − 10x = 0

2x(x−5)=02x=0 or x−5=0x=0 or x=5

Important: dividing by x would lose the valid solution x=0. Factor instead.

06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — DIFFERENCE OF SQUARES

Solve: 9x² − 25 = 0

(3x−5)(3x+5)=03x−5=0 or 3x+5=0x=5/3 or x=−5/3

07 · COMMON MISTAKES
Factoring before setting the equation equal to zero

The zero-product property only applies when the product equals 0.

Keeping only one root

A quadratic can have two distinct solutions. Solve every factor equation.

Dividing by a variable factor

That can erase a zero solution. Keep the factor and use the zero-product property.

Stopping before factoring completely

Take out a GCF first and continue until the remaining factors cannot be simplified by the available patterns.

08 · EXAM STRATEGY

Check whether factoring is the efficient method.

After writing the equation in standard form, look for a GCF, a trinomial with manageable integer factors, or a difference of squares. If the quadratic does not factor conveniently, another solving method may be more efficient. Always verify the requested solutions in the original equation.

10 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Rewrite the quadratic equation with 0 on one side.
  2. Factor completely.
  3. Set every factor equal to zero.
  4. Solve each factor equation.
  5. Check every solution in the original equation.
  6. Use Desmos to verify zeros when that adds speed or confidence.
