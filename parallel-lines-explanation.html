Parallel Lines
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Recognize and write equations of parallel lines.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to identify parallel lines from equations, tables, graphs, and points, and write the equation of a line parallel to a given line through a specified point.

y = 3x + 2∥y = 3x − 7
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Distinct parallel lines have equal slopes.

Parallel nonvertical lines rise and run at the same rate, so their slopes are equal. Their y-intercepts must be different if the lines are distinct.

Nonvertical linesm₁ = m₂Equal slopes mean the same direction.
Distinct linesb₁ ≠ b₂Different intercepts keep the lines separate.
Vertical linesx = a, x = cAny two distinct vertical lines are parallel.
CheckSame slopeRewrite equations if necessary before comparing.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Parallel Lines

Coplanar lines that never intersect.

same slope

Slope

The rate of change of y with respect to x.

m = Δy/Δx

Y-Intercept

The y-value where a nonvertical line crosses the y-axis.

(0, b)
04 · CORE RULE / FORMULA

Parallel lines share a slope.

m_parallel = m_given

To write a parallel line through a point, keep the given slope and use the point to determine the new intercept.

05 · HOW IT WORKS

Use a four-step process.

01

02

06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — IDENTIFYING PARALLEL LINES

Are y = 4x − 1 and y = 4x + 9 parallel?

m₁ = 4m₂ = 44 = 4

Yes. They have the same slope and different y-intercepts, so they are distinct parallel lines.

07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — STANDARD FORM

Is 2x + y = 7 parallel to 4x + 2y = 3?

2x + y = 7 → y = −2x + 74x + 2y = 3 → 2y = −4x + 3 → y = −2x + 3/2m₁ = m₂ = −2

Yes. Rewriting both equations reveals equal slopes.

11 · WORKED EXAMPLE 6 — EXAM STYLE

A line passes through (−2, 6) and is parallel to 3x − 2y = 8. Find its equation in slope-intercept form.

3x − 2y = 8 → −2y = −3x + 8y = (3/2)x − 4m = 3/26 = (3/2)(−2) + b = −3 + bb = 9y = (3/2)x + 9

12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Changing the sign of the slope

Parallel lines keep the same slope. Changing the sign usually changes the direction.

Comparing coefficients before isolating y

In standard form, the visible coefficients are not automatically the slopes.

Using the same intercept automatically

Equal slopes create parallel direction, but the new point determines the new intercept.

Ignoring vertical lines

All distinct vertical lines are parallel even though their slopes are undefined.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Extract the slope before doing anything else.

When a problem says parallel, your first target is the slope. Rewrite the given equation if needed, copy that slope, then use the given point to finish the new equation.

parallel→same slope
