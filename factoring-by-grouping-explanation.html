Factoring by Grouping
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Factor four-term polynomials by creating a shared binomial factor.

Grouping is the distributive property in reverse. Pair the terms, factor the greatest common factor from each pair, then factor out the binomial that appears in both groups.

x³ + 3x² + 2x + 6→(x + 3)(x² + 2)
02 · CORE METHOD

Two groups should produce the same binomial.

  1. Group the four terms into two pairs.
  2. Factor the GCF from each pair.
  3. Check that the remaining binomial factors match exactly.
  4. Factor out the shared binomial.
  5. Factor further if any remaining factor can still be simplified.
AB + AC + DB + DC= A(B+C) + D(B+C)= (A+D)(B+C)
03 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC GROUPING

Factor: x³ + 3x² + 2x + 6

(x³+3x²)+(2x+6)x²(x+3)+2(x+3)(x+3)(x²+2)
Math cross-check(x+3)(x²+2)=x³+3x²+2x+6.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — COEFFICIENTS AND SIGNS

Factor: 6x³ − 9x² + 4x − 6

(6x³−9x²)+(4x−6)3x²(2x−3)+2(2x−3)(2x−3)(3x²+2)
Math cross-check(2x−3)(3x²+2)=6x³−9x²+4x−6.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — FACTOR COMPLETELY

Factor: x³ + x² − 4x − 4

x²(x+1)−4(x+1)(x+1)(x²−4)(x+1)(x−2)(x+2)

Do not stop early: after grouping, x²−4 is still a difference of squares.

Math cross-check(x+1)(x−2)(x+2)=x³+x²−4x−4.
06 · REORDERING CAN REVEAL THE GROUPS

Factor: 3x³ + 6x + 2x² + 4

The useful pairs are easier to see after reordering the middle terms.

3x³+2x²+6x+4x²(3x+2)+2(3x+2)(3x+2)(x²+2)

Reordering terms does not change the polynomial because addition is commutative.

Math cross-check(3x+2)(x²+2)=3x³+2x²+6x+4, the same terms as the original expression.
07 · WHEN A PAIRING DOES NOT WORK

Do not force mismatched binomials.

For x³+2x²+5x+6, the first pair gives x²(x+2), but the second pair does not factor to the same (x+2). Try a sensible reorder if one is available; otherwise another factoring method may be needed.

x²(x+2) + (5x+6)→no shared binomial
08 · WHY GROUPING WORKS

It is the distributive property in reverse.

A(B)+C(B)=(A+C)B

After the GCF is removed from each pair, the repeated binomial acts like the common factor B.

09 · COMMON MISTAKES
Forgetting parentheses around each group

Keep each pair together before taking out its GCF.

Losing a negative sign

If you factor −2 from −2x−6, the result is −2(x+3).

Stopping before the shared binomial is factored out

x²(x+3)+2(x+3) is an intermediate step, not the final factorization.

Not factoring completely

After grouping, inspect the remaining factor for patterns such as a difference of squares.

Forcing two different binomials to match

The shared binomial must be exactly the same expression in both groups.

10 · EXAM STRATEGY

Scan, group, match, then check.

With four terms, first look for a pairing that gives useful GCFs. After factoring each pair, the remaining binomial should match exactly. Expand your final answer for a fast algebra check. For a cubic with linear factors, Desmos can also verify the zeros when that is useful.

12 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Grouping is reverse distribution.
  2. Pair terms and factor a GCF from each pair.
  3. The two groups should reveal the same binomial factor.
  4. Factor out that shared binomial.
  5. Continue factoring until no familiar factor remains.
  6. Expand to verify; use Desmos as a check when roots give useful confirmation.
