Slope from a Graph
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Read slope directly from a graph.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to choose two points on a line, measure the vertical and horizontal changes, calculate slope, and interpret its sign and size.

rise ÷ run→m = Δy/Δx
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Slope measures how a line changes as you move from left to right.

Pick two clear points that lie exactly on the line. From the first point to the second, find the vertical change and horizontal change. Divide the vertical change by the horizontal change.

RiseΔyVertical change.
RunΔxHorizontal change.
Positive sloperises righty increases as x increases.
Negative slopefalls righty decreases as x increases.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Slope

The rate of vertical change compared with horizontal change.

m = Δy/Δx

Rise

The change in y between two points.

y₂ − y₁

Run

The change in x between the same two points.

x₂ − x₁
04 · CORE RULE / FORMULA

Use the same two points and the same subtraction order.

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

You can count rise and run on the graph or substitute coordinates into the formula. Both methods must give the same result.

05 · HOW IT WORKS

Use a four-step graph method.

01
Choose two exact pointsPrefer grid intersections or labeled points.
02
Count the riseMove vertically from the first y-value to the second.
03
Count the runMove horizontally between the matching x-values.
04
DivideCompute rise/run and simplify.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

A line passes through (1, 2) and (4, 8). Find its slope.

rise = 8 − 2 = 6run = 4 − 1 = 3m = 6/3 = 2
MeaningFor each 1-unit increase in x, y increases by 2.
06B · VERIFIED GRAPH

See the rise and run on the actual line.

The two worked-example points lie on y = 2x. The graph below is generated from that equation, not drawn by eye.

Math cross-check: 2 = 2(1), 4 = 2(2), and 8 = 2(4). All three verified points satisfy y = 2x.

07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE SLOPE

A line passes through (−2, 5) and (2, −3). Find its slope.

rise = −3 − 5 = −8run = 2 − (−2) = 4m = −8/4 = −2

Visual check: The line falls as you move right, so a negative slope is reasonable.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — FRACTIONAL SLOPE

A line passes through (0, 1) and (6, 4). Find its slope.

rise = 4 − 1 = 3run = 6 − 0 = 6m = 3/6 = 1/2

A slope of 1/2 means the line rises 1 unit for every 2 units moved right.

09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — HORIZONTAL LINE

A horizontal line passes through (−3, 4) and (5, 4). Find its slope.

rise = 4 − 4 = 0run = 5 − (−3) = 8m = 0/8 = 0
Key factEvery horizontal line has slope 0.
10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — VERTICAL LINE

A vertical line passes through (3, −1) and (3, 7). What is its slope?

rise = 7 − (−1) = 8run = 3 − 3 = 0m = 8/0

Conclusion: Division by zero is undefined, so every vertical line has undefined slope.

11 · WORKED EXAMPLE 6 — EXAM STYLE

A graph of water volume versus time passes through (2, 18) and (7, 43), where time is in minutes and volume is in liters. Find and interpret the slope.

Δvolume = 43 − 18 = 25 LΔtime = 7 − 2 = 5 minm = 25/5 = 5 L/min
InterpretationThe volume increases by 5 liters per minute.
DESMOS STRATEGY

Use two exact points to verify a slope quickly.

Enter

Enter y=2x, then add the points (1,2) and (4,8).

Look for

Both points should lie exactly on the same line. The line rises 2 units for each 1 unit moved right.

Use it to answer

Use the change between the two points: (8−2)/(4−1)=6/3=2, confirming slope m=2.

Why it works

Slope is the constant ratio of vertical change to horizontal change for any two distinct points on a nonvertical line.

Faster or not?Usually not when two clean graph points are already visible. Rise over run is faster; Desmos is useful for checking a hard-to-read graph or verifying that chosen points fit the same line.

Math cross-check: From (1,2) and (4,8), Δy=6 and Δx=3, so m=2. Substitution into y=2x confirms both points.

12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Using points that are not exactly on the line

Choose clear grid intersections or labeled points whenever possible.

Reversing only one subtraction

If you switch the y subtraction order, switch the x subtraction order too.

Forgetting the sign

A line that falls to the right must have negative slope.

Calling a vertical line slope 0

Horizontal lines have slope 0. Vertical lines have undefined slope.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Use the cleanest two points you can read.

Do not estimate from the line's angle when exact points are available. Pick two precise points, count rise and run, simplify the ratio, and use the graph direction as a sign check.

rise = −6, run = 3→m = −2

15 · LESSON RECAP

Slope from a graph is rise divided by run.

  1. Choose two exact points on the line.
  2. Find the vertical change, Δy.
  3. Find the horizontal change, Δx.
  4. Divide and simplify: m = Δy/Δx.
  5. Use the graph direction to check the sign.
positive: rises rightnegative: falls righthorizontal: 0vertical: undefined
