Quadratic Formula

01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Solve any quadratic equation using one reliable formula.

When a quadratic is written as ax²+bx+c=0, the quadratic formula gives its solutions directly from the coefficients.

02 · CORE RULE

Identify a, b, and c first.

x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / (2a)

The expression b²−4ac is the discriminant. It tells you how many real solutions the equation has.

Positiveb²−4ac > 0Two distinct real solutions
Zerob²−4ac = 0One repeated real solution
Negativeb²−4ac < 0No real solutions
03 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1

Solve: 2x²−7x+3=0

a=2, b=−7, c=3x=(7±√(49−24))/4x=(7±5)/4x=3 or x=1/2
Math cross-checkAt x=3: 18−21+3=0. At x=1/2: 1/2−7/2+3=0.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — IRRATIONAL ROOTS

Solve: x²−4x−1=0

a=1, b=−4, c=−1x=(4±√(16+4))/2x=(4±2√5)/2x=2±√5
Math cross-checkFor x=2±√5, (x−2)²=5, which is equivalent to x²−4x−1=0.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — ONE REAL ROOT

Solve: x²−6x+9=0

b²−4ac=36−36=0x=(6±0)/2x=3
Math cross-check3²−6(3)+9=0.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — NO REAL ROOTS

Solve over the real numbers: x²+2x+5=0

b²−4ac=4−20=−16No real solution

Why: the square root of a negative number is not real.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES
Using the wrong sign for b

If the equation is 2x²−7x+3=0, then b=−7, so −b=7.

Forgetting parentheses in the denominator

The entire numerator is divided by 2a.

Dropping ±

A positive discriminant produces two real solutions.

Using the formula before standard form

Move every term to one side so the other side is zero before identifying a, b, and c.

08 · EXAM STRATEGY

Write the coefficients before substituting.

On an exam, first rewrite the equation in standard form, then write a=, b=, and c=. This small step prevents most sign errors. Keep radicals exact unless the question asks for a decimal.

10 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Write the equation as ax²+bx+c=0.
  2. Identify a, b, and c with their signs.
  3. Substitute into the quadratic formula.
  4. Simplify the discriminant carefully.
  5. Keep both ± branches when they are real.
  6. Verify solutions in the original equation when practical.
