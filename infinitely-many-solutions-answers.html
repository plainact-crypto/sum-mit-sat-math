Infinitely Many Solutions

Solutions correspond exactly to Practice Questions 1–18.

01 · SKILL CHECK

1. Compare equivalent slope-intercept equations

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Divide 2y = 4x + 6 by 2 to get y = 2x + 3. Both equations describe the same line, so every point on that line satisfies both.

2. Recognize a scaled equation

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Multiplying x + y = 5 by 2 gives 2x + 2y = 10 exactly. The equations have the same solution set.

3. Check proportional coefficients

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Multiplying 3x − y = 7 by 2 gives 6x − 2y = 14. The second equation is the same line.
02 · CORE PRACTICE

4. Match slope and intercept

Answer: C) 4
For y = 4x − 1 and y = kx − 1 to be identical, their slopes must match. Therefore k = 4.

5. Scale every term

Answer: C) 18
The second left side is twice the first: 4x + 6y = 2(2x + 3y). The right side must also double, so c = 18.

6. Identify a negative multiple

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Multiply x − 2y = 4 by −3 to get −3x + 6y = −12. The equations are identical after scaling.

7. Rewrite to compare the lines

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
From x + 2y = 14, divide by 2 after isolating y: y = −0.5x + 7. This matches the first equation exactly.

8. Compare an equation with its multiple

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Doubling 2x − y = 5 gives 4x − 2y = 10. Both equations represent the same line.

9. Verify a factor of three

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Three times 3x + y = 8 is 9x + 3y = 24. Every solution of one equation is a solution of the other.

10. Convert to the same form

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Starting with y = 3x + 2, multiply by −2: −2y = −6x − 4, which rearranges to 6x − 2y = −4. The equations are equivalent.

11. Check proportional standard-form equations

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Multiplying 5x + 2y = 12 by 3 gives 15x + 6y = 36. The lines coincide.
03 · EXAM-STYLE PRACTICE

12. Interpret two versions of the same pricing rule

Answer: D) Infinitely many
Doubling C = 12 + 4n gives 2C = 24 + 8n exactly. Every ordered pair on the pricing rule satisfies both equations.

13. Determine the matching parameter

Answer: B) 3
The second equation must be twice the first. Doubling 2x + ky = 6 gives 4x + 2ky = 12. Match 2k = 6, so k = 3.

14. Match two slope-intercept forms

Answer: C) 5
Divide 2y = 8x + 16 by 2 to get y = 4x + 8. Therefore p − 1 = 4, so p = 5.

15. Match standard-form coefficients

Answer: B) 3
Doubling ax + 2y = 7 gives 2ax + 4y = 14. Matching 6x + 4y = 14 requires 2a = 6, so a = 3.

16. Compare lines from pairs of points

Answer: C) Infinitely many solutions
Line A has slope (6−2)/(2−0) = 2 and equation y = 2x + 2. Line B has slope (8−4)/(3−1) = 2, and (1,4) satisfies y = 2x + 2. They are the same line.
04 · CHALLENGE PROBLEMS

17. Recover a hidden coefficient

Answer: C) 4
The equations already have the same y-coefficient and constant, so their x-coefficients must match: a − 2 = 2. Hence a = 4.

18. Recover a point on the shared line

Answer: B) −2
Line 1 has slope (12−4)/(5−1) = 2, so using (1,4) gives y = 2x + 2. At x = −2, this line has y = −2. Thus q = −2. The other Line 2 point (2,6) also satisfies y = 2x + 2.
