Perpendicular Lines
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Recognize and write equations of perpendicular lines.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to identify perpendicular lines from slopes and equations, find a perpendicular slope, and write the equation of a perpendicular line through a specified point.

m₁ = 2⟂m₂ = −1/2
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle.

For two nonvertical, nonhorizontal perpendicular lines, their slopes are negative reciprocals. That means you flip the fraction and change the sign.

Nonzero finite slopesm₁m₂ = −1The product of perpendicular slopes is −1.
Negative reciprocala/b → −b/aFlip the fraction and reverse the sign.
Horizontal linem = 0Its perpendicular line is vertical.
Vertical lineslope undefinedIts perpendicular line is horizontal.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Perpendicular Lines

Lines that intersect to form a 90° angle.

right angle

Reciprocal

The result of swapping a nonzero fraction's numerator and denominator.

a/b → b/a

Negative Reciprocal

The reciprocal with the opposite sign.

a/b → −b/a
04 · CORE RULE / FORMULA

Multiply perpendicular slopes to get −1.

m₁m₂ = −1

Equivalently, if the given slope is m, the perpendicular slope is −1/m, provided m is neither zero nor undefined.

05 · HOW IT WORKS

Use a four-step process.

01
Find the given slopeRewrite the equation in y = mx + b form if needed.
02
Take the negative reciprocalFlip numerator and denominator, then change the sign.
03
Use the required pointSubstitute into y = mx + b or use point-slope form.
04
VerifyCheck the point and confirm the slope product is −1.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — IDENTIFYING PERPENDICULAR LINES

Are y = 3x + 4 and y = (−1/3)x + 7 perpendicular?

m₁ = 3m₂ = −1/3m₁m₂ = 3(−1/3) = −1

Yes. Their slopes are negative reciprocals, so the lines are perpendicular.

07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — STANDARD FORM

Is 2x + 3y = 6 perpendicular to 3x − 2y = 8?

2x + 3y = 6 → y = (−2/3)x + 23x − 2y = 8 → y = (3/2)x − 4(−2/3)(3/2) = −1

Yes. Rewriting both equations reveals negative-reciprocal slopes.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — WRITE A PERPENDICULAR LINE

Write the line perpendicular to y = 2x + 1 through (4, 3).

given slope = 2perpendicular slope = −1/23 = (−1/2)(4) + b3 = −2 + b → b = 5y = (−1/2)x + 5
CheckAt x = 4, y = −2 + 5 = 3, and 2(−1/2) = −1.
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — NEGATIVE FRACTIONAL SLOPE

Write the line perpendicular to y = (−3/5)x + 4 through (5, 1).

given slope = −3/5perpendicular slope = 5/31 = (5/3)(5) + b1 = 25/3 + b → b = −22/3y = (5/3)x − 22/3
10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — HORIZONTAL AND VERTICAL

Write the line perpendicular to x = 6 through (−2, 9).

x = 6 is verticala perpendicular line must be horizontalthe point has y-coordinate 9y = 9

Horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular even though the usual slope-product rule cannot be applied to an undefined slope.

12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Changing only the sign

The perpendicular slope to 2 is −1/2, not −2.

Taking only the reciprocal

The perpendicular slope to 3/4 is −4/3, not 4/3.

Reading standard-form coefficients as slopes

Rewrite the equation or calculate its slope before applying the perpendicular rule.

Forcing the product rule onto vertical lines

A vertical line has undefined slope; use the horizontal-vertical relationship instead.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Translate “perpendicular” into “negative reciprocal.”

Once you know the given slope, write the perpendicular slope immediately. Then use any required point to determine the equation. For horizontal or vertical lines, use their special relationship directly.

perpendicular→negative reciprocal slope

15 · LESSON RECAP

Perpendicular lines turn the slope by a right angle.

  1. Find or calculate the given slope.
  2. Take its negative reciprocal.
  3. Use the required point to find the equation.
  4. Remember that horizontal and vertical lines are perpendicular.
  5. Verify the point and, when applicable, the slope product of −1.
