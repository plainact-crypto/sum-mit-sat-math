Completing the Square
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Rewrite a quadratic so one side becomes a perfect square.

Completing the square turns an expression such as x²+6x into a squared binomial by adding the exact value needed to make a perfect-square trinomial.

x²+6x→(x+3)²
02 · CORE RULE

For x²+bx, add (b/2)².

x²+bx+(b/2)²=(x+b/2)²
  1. Move the constant term if needed.
  2. Make the coefficient of x² equal to 1 before completing the square.
  3. Take half the coefficient of x.
  4. Square that number.
  5. Add the same value to both sides of the equation.
  6. Factor the perfect-square trinomial and solve.
03 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Solve: x²+6x+5=0

x²+6x=−5x²+6x+9=4(x+3)²=4x+3=±2x=−1 or x=−5
Math cross-checkAt x=−1: 1−6+5=0. At x=−5: 25−30+5=0.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — LEADING COEFFICIENT

Solve: 2x²−12x+10=0

x²−6x+5=0x²−6x=−5x²−6x+9=4(x−3)²=4x=1 or x=5
Math cross-checkFor x=1, 2−12+10=0. For x=5, 50−60+10=0.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — CONSTANT ON THE OTHER SIDE

Solve: x²+4x=12

x²+4x+4=16(x+2)²=16x+2=±4x=2 or x=−6
Math cross-check2²+4(2)=12 and (−6)²+4(−6)=12.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — IRRATIONAL SOLUTIONS

Solve: x²−2x−1=0

x²−2x=1x²−2x+1=2(x−1)²=2x=1±√2
Math cross-checkIf x=1±√2, then x−1=±√2, so (x−1)²=2 and the transformed equation is true.
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — NO REAL SOLUTION

Solve over the real numbers: x²+2x+5=0

x²+2x=−5x²+2x+1=−4(x+1)²=−4No real solution

Why: a real square cannot be negative.

08 · COMMON MISTAKES
Using b² instead of (b/2)²

For x²+8x, half of 8 is 4, so add 16.

Adding to only one side

An equation must stay balanced. Whatever you add on the left must also be added on the right.

Completing the square before making x² monic

For 2x²+8x=6, divide by 2 first to get x²+4x=3.

Forgetting ± after taking square roots

From (x−3)²=9, write x−3=±3.

09 · EXAM STRATEGY

Use the half-and-square pattern deliberately.

When the quadratic is not easy to factor, completing the square gives a systematic route and keeps exact radical answers visible. If the leading coefficient is not 1, normalize the equation first whenever possible.

11 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Isolate the variable terms.
  2. Make the x² coefficient 1.
  3. Add (b/2)² to both sides.
  4. Factor the left side as a square.
  5. Take square roots with ±.
  6. Solve and verify in the original equation.
