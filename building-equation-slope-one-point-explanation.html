Building an Equation from Slope and One Point
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Write a linear equation when you know the slope and one point.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to use a given slope and a point on a line to build its equation, simplify it when useful, and verify that the point satisfies the result.

m = 3, point (2,5)→y = 3x − 1
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

One slope and one point determine a unique nonvertical line.

Point-slope form is designed for this situation. If the slope is m and the known point is (x₁,y₁), write y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).

SlopemRate of change.
Known point(x₁,y₁)A point the line must pass through.
Point-slope formy − y₁ = m(x − x₁)Direct construction form.
CheckSubstitute the pointBoth sides must match.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Point-Slope Form

A form of a linear equation using one point and the slope.

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Slope

The change in y for each 1-unit change in x.

m = Δy / Δx

Equivalent Equations

Different algebraic forms that describe the same line.

y − 5 = 3(x − 2) ↔ y = 3x − 1
04 · CORE RULE / FORMULA

Substitute the slope and coordinates into point-slope form.

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Be especially careful when a coordinate is negative: subtracting a negative becomes addition.

05 · HOW IT WORKS

Use a four-step process.

01
Identify the dataWrite down m and (x₁,y₁).
02
SubstitutePlace the values into y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
03
Simplify if requestedDistribute and solve for y to reach slope-intercept form.
04
VerifySubstitute the known point into the final equation.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Write the equation of the line with slope 3 through (2,5).

y − 5 = 3(x − 2)y − 5 = 3x − 6y = 3x − 1
CheckAt x = 2, y = 3(2) − 1 = 5 ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE X-COORDINATE

Write the equation with slope −2 through (−1,4).

y − 4 = −2(x − (−1))y − 4 = −2(x + 1)y = −2x + 2

Sign check: x − (−1) becomes x + 1.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — FRACTIONAL SLOPE

A line has slope 1/2 and passes through (6,−1). Write its equation.

y + 1 = (1/2)(x − 6)y + 1 = (1/2)x − 3y = (1/2)x − 4
CheckAt x = 6, y = 3 − 4 = −1 ✓
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — ZERO SLOPE

A line has slope 0 and passes through (3,7). Write its equation.

y − 7 = 0(x − 3)y − 7 = 0y = 7

A zero slope produces a horizontal line, so y stays constant.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — FROM A CONTEXT

A quantity grows at 12 units per hour. After 4 hours its value is 58. Write a linear model y for the value after x hours.

m = 12, point (4,58)y − 58 = 12(x − 4)y = 12x + 10
CheckAt x = 4, y = 48 + 10 = 58.
11 · WORKED EXAMPLE 6 — EXAM STYLE

A line has slope −3/4 and contains the point (8,2). Which slope-intercept equation represents the line?

y − 2 = (−3/4)(x − 8)y − 2 = (−3/4)x + 6y = (−3/4)x + 8

12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Using x₁ and y₁ in the wrong places

The x-coordinate belongs inside the parentheses with x; the y-coordinate belongs with y.

Dropping the subtraction signs

The form is y − y₁ = m(x − x₁). Keep the structure before simplifying.

Mishandling negative coordinates

If x₁ = −3, then x − x₁ becomes x + 3.

Forgetting to verify

The known point must satisfy the final equation exactly.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Use the given point immediately instead of hunting for the intercept.

When a problem gives a slope and one point, point-slope form is usually the fastest reliable setup. Convert to another form only if the question asks for it or the choices are written that way.

m + one point→point-slope form first

15 · LESSON RECAP

Slope plus one point is enough to build the line.

  1. Identify m and (x₁,y₁).
  2. Start with y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
  3. Substitute carefully, especially with negative coordinates.
  4. Simplify into the requested form.
  5. Verify by substituting the known point.
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)m = slope(x₁,y₁) = known point
