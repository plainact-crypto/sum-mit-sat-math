Slope from Two Points
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Find the slope of a line from any two distinct points.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to use two ordered pairs to calculate slope, simplify the result exactly, recognize zero and undefined slope, and interpret slope as a rate of change.

(x₁, y₁), (x₂, y₂)→m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Slope compares vertical change with horizontal change.

Given two points on the same line, subtract their y-coordinates to find the vertical change and subtract their x-coordinates in the same order to find the horizontal change. Then divide.

Point 1(x₁, y₁)Choose either point first.
Point 2(x₂, y₂)Use the other point second.
Risey₂ − y₁Vertical change.
Runx₂ − x₁Horizontal change.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Slope

The change in y divided by the corresponding change in x.

m = Δy/Δx

Ordered Pair

A point written with its x-coordinate first and y-coordinate second.

(x, y)

Rate of Change

How much the output changes for each one-unit change in the input.

change in output / change in input
04 · CORE RULE / FORMULA

Keep the subtraction order consistent.

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

You may label either point as point 1. Reversing both differences gives the same slope. Reversing only one difference changes the sign and gives an incorrect result.

05 · HOW IT WORKS

Use the same four steps every time.

01
Label the pointsWrite (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂).
02
Subtract the y-valuesCompute y₂ − y₁.
03
Subtract the x-valuesCompute x₂ − x₁ in the same order.
04
Divide and simplifyReduce the ratio to an integer or exact fraction when possible.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Find the slope through (2, 5) and (6, 13).

m = (13 − 5)/(6 − 2)m = 8/4m = 2
CheckFrom x = 2 to x = 6, x rises by 4 while y rises by 8, so the rate is 8/4 = 2. ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE SLOPE

Find the slope through (−3, 7) and (1, −5).

m = (−5 − 7)/(1 − (−3))m = −12/4m = −3

Interpretation: As x increases by 1, y decreases by 3.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — FRACTIONAL SLOPE

Find the slope through (−2, 4) and (4, 7).

m = (7 − 4)/(4 − (−2))m = 3/6m = 1/2

Simplify the slope exactly. The fraction 1/2 means y rises 1 unit for every 2 units x increases.

09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — ZERO SLOPE

Find the slope through (−4, 9) and (5, 9).

m = (9 − 9)/(5 − (−4))m = 0/9m = 0
MeaningThe y-value does not change, so the line is horizontal.
10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — UNDEFINED SLOPE

Find the slope through (3, −2) and (3, 8).

m = (8 − (−2))/(3 − 3)m = 10/0m is undefined

Important: Division by zero is undefined. Equal x-coordinates create a vertical line.

11 · WORKED EXAMPLE 6 — EXAM STYLE

A tank contains 84 liters of water at 2 minutes and 60 liters at 8 minutes. Find the rate of change of the amount of water.

(2, 84) and (8, 60)m = (60 − 84)/(8 − 2)m = −24/6m = −4 liters per minute
InterpretationThe amount of water decreases by 4 liters each minute.

Why it works

Two distinct nonvertical points determine one line, and the slope is the constant ratio of vertical change to horizontal change along that line.

Faster or not?

The slope formula is usually faster for two clean points. Desmos is useful for checking sign, order, and whether both points fit the same line.

Math cross-check: (13−5)/(6−2)=2, and both points satisfy y = 2x + 1.

12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Mixing x- and y-coordinates

Use y-values in the numerator and x-values in the denominator.

Changing subtraction order halfway

If you start with y₂ − y₁, use x₂ − x₁ below it.

Forgetting a negative inside parentheses

For 1 − (−3), the result is 4, not −2.

Calling a vertical slope zero

A horizontal line has slope 0. A vertical line has undefined slope.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Write the formula before substituting coordinates.

A short setup prevents coordinate-order errors. Label the points, substitute carefully, simplify signs in the numerator and denominator separately, then reduce the final ratio.

(−1, 6), (5, −3)→m = −9/6 = −3/2

15 · LESSON RECAP

Two points contain everything you need to find slope.

  1. Label the two ordered pairs.
  2. Subtract the y-coordinates.
  3. Subtract the x-coordinates in the same order.
  4. Divide and simplify exactly.
  5. Remember: horizontal means slope 0; vertical means undefined slope.
Δy = y₂ − y₁Δx = x₂ − x₁m = Δy/Δx
