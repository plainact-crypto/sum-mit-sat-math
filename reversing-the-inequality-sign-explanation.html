Reversing the Inequality Sign
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Know exactly when and why an inequality sign must reverse.

When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, the inequality direction reverses. This keeps the comparison true after the order of the numbers is flipped.

−2x > 6→x < −3
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Positive operations preserve order; negative scaling reverses order.

Adding or subtracting the same value on both sides does not reverse the sign. Multiplying or dividing by a positive value also keeps the sign. The reversal happens only when the multiplier or divisor is negative.

Add / subtractNo reversalExample: x−4<7 → x<11.
× or ÷ positiveNo reversalExample: 3x>12 → x>4.
× or ÷ negativeReverseExample: −2x>6 → x<−3.
03 · WHY THE SIGN REVERSES

Multiplying by a negative reflects numbers across zero.

Start with the true statement 2<5. Multiply both sides by −1: the numbers become −2 and −5. But −2 is greater than −5, so the equivalent statement is −2>−5.

Order check2<5 is true. After multiplying by −1, writing −2<−5 would be false. Reversing the sign gives −2>−5, which is true.
04 · CORE RULE

Reverse the inequality only when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.

NEGATIVE × OR ÷ → FLIP THE SIGN

06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE DIVISOR

Solve x/(−4) ≤ 3.

x/(−4) ≤ 3x ≥ −12

Why? Multiplying both sides by −4 reverses ≤ to ≥.

Checkx=−12 gives 3≤3, true. x=−16 gives 4≤3, false.

Solve 5−3x ≥ 17.

Important: Subtracting 5 did not reverse the sign. Dividing by −3 did.

Checkx=−5 gives 5−3(−5)=20≥17, true. x=−3 gives 14≥17, false.

09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — EXAM-STYLE CONTEXT

A freezer is at −2°C and cools by 3°C each hour. After h hours, its temperature is T=−2−3h. For what values of h is T below −14°C?

−2−3h < −14−3h < −12h > 4

Interpretation: The freezer is below −14°C only after more than 4 hours.

Context checkAt h=5, T=−17°C, which is below −14°C. At h=4, T=−14°C, not below it.

11 · COMMON MISTAKES
Reversing after subtraction

Subtracting a negative number is not the trigger. The sign reverses only for multiplication or division by a negative number.

Forgetting the reversal

From −5x>20, the correct result is x<−4, not x>−4.

Reversing twice

If there is only one negative multiplication or division step, reverse only once.

Checking only the boundary

A strict inequality excludes the boundary, but you should also test a value your final answer says is allowed.

12 · EXAM STRATEGY

Circle or mentally flag the negative divisor before you divide.

In a timed school or exam setting, most errors happen in the final isolation step. Before dividing, look at the coefficient attached to the variable. If it is negative, reverse the inequality sign at that exact step.

14 · LESSON RECAP

One trigger controls the whole rule.

  1. Adding or subtracting the same value does not reverse an inequality.
  2. Multiplying or dividing by a positive value does not reverse it.
  3. Multiplying or dividing by a negative value does reverse it.
  4. The reversal preserves the correct order after negative scaling.
  5. Verify the result in the original inequality.
