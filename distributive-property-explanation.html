Distributive Property
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Expand and rewrite expressions without changing their value.

The distributive property lets one factor outside parentheses multiply every term inside. It is a core tool for expanding expressions, handling negative signs, and preparing expressions for combining like terms.

4(x + 3)→4x + 12
02 · CORE RULE

Multiply the outside factor by every term inside the parentheses.

a(b + c) = ab + ac
a(b - c) = ab - ac

Key idea: the sign travels with each term. A negative outside factor changes the sign of every product it creates.

03 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Expand: 4(x + 3)

4(x + 3)4·x + 4·34x + 12
Value checkAt x=2, the original is 4(5)=20 and the expanded form is 8+12=20.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — TWO-TERM EXPRESSION

Expand: 5(2m - 7)

5·2m + 5·(-7)10m - 35

Think of subtraction as adding a negative term. That makes the sign easier to track.

Value checkAt m=4, both expressions equal 5.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — NEGATIVE OUTSIDE FACTOR

Expand: -3(2y - 5)

(-3)(2y) + (-3)(-5)-6y + 15

The second product is positive because a negative times a negative is positive.

Value checkAt y=2, the original and expanded expressions both equal 3.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — FRACTION FACTOR

Expand: 1/2(6p + 8)

(1/2)·6p + (1/2)·83p + 4
Value checkAt p=2, both expressions equal 10.
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — DISTRIBUTE, THEN COMBINE

Simplify: 3(2x + 5) - 4x

6x + 15 - 4x2x + 15

Distribution comes first. Then combine the like x-terms.

Math cross-checkAt x=3, the original and simplified expressions both equal 21.
08 · REVERSE DISTRIBUTION

Factoring undoes distribution.

If every term has a common factor, you can pull that factor outside parentheses.

15x + 205(3x + 4)
Value checkAt x=2, both forms equal 50.
09 · A RELIABLE METHOD
  1. Identify the factor immediately outside the parentheses.
  2. Multiply it by the first term inside.
  3. Multiply it by every remaining term, carrying each sign carefully.
  4. Remove the parentheses only after every term has been multiplied.
  5. Combine like terms if the expression still has any.
10 · COMMON MISTAKES
Distributing to only the first term

3(x+4) is 3x+12, not 3x+4.

Losing a negative sign

-2(x-5) becomes -2x+10. The outside factor multiplies both terms.

Adding coefficients instead of multiplying

4(3x) is 12x, not 7x.

Changing exponents without multiplication of variables

3(x²+2x) becomes 3x²+6x; distribution does not change the exponents.

11 · EXAM STRATEGY

Mark each product before simplifying.

In an exam-style problem, mentally draw an arrow from the outside factor to every term inside the parentheses. Finish all products first, then combine like terms. If signs are messy, test one convenient value to confirm the two forms are equivalent.

13 · LESSON RECAP
  1. The outside factor multiplies every term inside the parentheses.
  2. Carry negative signs with the terms they belong to.
  3. After distributing, combine only like terms.
  4. Factoring is the reverse use of the distributive property.
  5. A quick substitution can verify that two expressions are equivalent.
