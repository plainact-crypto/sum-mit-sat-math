Systems Word Problems
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Translate a situation into two equations, solve the system, and interpret the answer.

Systems word problems describe two unknown quantities connected by two facts. Define the variables, turn each fact into an equation, solve, and answer with the correct units.

02 · BUILD THE MODEL

Name the quantities before writing equations.

Step 1Define variablesState what x and y represent.
Step 2Find the total relationshipUse the total count or combined amount.
Step 3Find the value relationshipUse prices, rates, percentages, or another weighted total.
Step 4Solve and interpretUse substitution or elimination, then answer exactly what was asked.
03 · MASTER EXAMPLE — TICKETS

A school event sold 18 adult and student tickets for $126. Adult tickets cost $9 and student tickets cost $5. How many adult tickets were sold?

Let x be adult tickets and y be student tickets.

x + y = 189x + 5y = 1265x + 5y = 904x = 36x = 9, y = 9
Check9+9=18 and 9(9)+5(9)=126, so 9 adult tickets were sold.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE — MIXTURE

Make 30 liters of a 38% solution by mixing 20% and 50% solutions. How many liters of the 50% solution are needed?

Let x be liters of 20% solution and y be liters of 50% solution.

x + y = 300.20x + 0.50y = 11.4x = 30-y6 + 0.30y = 11.4y = 18
Check12(0.20)+18(0.50)=2.4+9=11.4, exactly 38% of 30 L.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE — COMPARING PLANS

Plan A costs $20 to join plus $6 per month. Plan B costs $44 to join plus $3 per month. When do the costs match?

20 + 6m = 44 + 3m3m = 24m = 8
CheckBoth plans cost $68 after 8 months.
06 · CHOOSE A METHOD

The context builds the system; the coefficients suggest the method.

Substitutionwhen one variable isolates easilyA total such as x+y=30 often makes substitution quick.
Eliminationwhen coefficients line upMultiply one equation if needed, then add or subtract.
Graphing toolfor verificationEnter both equations and inspect their common solution.
07 · INTERPRETATION

An ordered pair is not the final answer until you connect it to the variables.

If x represents adult tickets and y represents student tickets, (9,9) means 9 adult tickets and 9 student tickets. If the question asks only for adult tickets, report 9 adult tickets.

08 · COMMON MISTAKES
Writing equations before defining variables

This can swap quantities or lead to answering the wrong variable.

Using prices without counts

A revenue equation needs price × number for each category.

Using 38 instead of 0.38

Percent equations need consistent decimal or fractional forms.

Skipping the context check

A negative count or impossible percentage signals an error.

09 · EXAM STRATEGY

Underline the two independent facts.

Most test-style systems word problems give one equation from a total and a second from a weighted total, rate, or comparison. Build both equations before solving.

11 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Define both variables with units.
  2. Translate two independent facts into two equations.
  3. Solve with substitution or elimination.
  4. Verify in both original equations.
  5. Interpret the result in context.
