Rewriting Equivalent Expressions
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Rewrite an expression without changing its value.

Equivalent expressions can look different but produce the same value for every allowed value of the variable. Rewriting means using valid algebra moves—distribution, combining like terms, or factoring—to expose a more useful form.

4(x + 3) - 2x→2x + 12
02 · CORE IDEA

Change the form, not the value.

Equivalent expressions give equal outputs for every allowed input.

Key test: each rewrite step must come from a valid algebra rule. Checking one input can catch an error, but it does not prove two expressions are equivalent for all inputs.

03 · THREE MAIN REWRITE TOOLS

Distribute

3(x+5) = 3x+15

Multiply the outside factor by every term inside.

Combine like terms

5x-2x+7 = 3x+7

Only terms with the same variable part can combine.

Factor

6y²+9y = 3y(2y+3)

Factoring reverses distribution and can reveal structure.

04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1

Rewrite 4(x + 3) - 2x in simplest expanded form.

4x + 12 - 2x2x + 12

Distribute first, then combine the two x-terms.

Math cross-checkAt x=5, the original is 4(8)-10=22 and 2(5)+12=22.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2

Rewrite 3a + 6 + 2(a - 1).

3a + 6 + 2a - 25a + 4

Keep the constant terms separate from the variable terms until the final combine step.

Math cross-checkAt a=2, both forms equal 14.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — FACTORED FORM

Rewrite 6y² + 9y by factoring the greatest common factor.

3y(2y + 3)

The factor 3y is common to both terms. Distributing it back gives 6y²+9y, so the forms are equivalent.

Math cross-checkAt y=2, both forms equal 42.
07 · HOW TO RECOGNIZE A BAD REWRITE
Incomplete distribution

3(x+2) is 3x+6, not 3x+2.

Combining unlike terms

2x+3x² cannot be combined into one term.

Losing a negative sign

-(x-4) becomes -x+4.

08 · EXAM STRATEGY

Rewrite toward the feature the question asks for.

Expanded form is often best for combining coefficients. Factored form is often best for identifying common factors or zeros later. In multiple-choice questions, simplify the original expression before comparing choices instead of testing random values alone.

10 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Equivalent expressions have the same value for every allowed input.
  2. Distribution, combining like terms, and factoring are valid rewrite tools.
  3. Track negative signs through every step.
  4. Choose expanded or factored form based on what the problem asks.
  5. Use substitution or Desmos as a verification tool after the algebra.
