Equations with Fractions
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Clear the fractions, then solve the linear equation.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to solve linear equations containing fractional coefficients or constants by multiplying every term by a common denominator, simplifying, and then using ordinary linear-equation steps.

x/3 + 2 = 6→x = 12
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Fractions do not change the algebra rules.

The fastest approach is often to remove denominators first. Multiply every term on both sides by the least common denominator, or LCD. Once the fractions disappear, solve the resulting linear equation normally.

FindThe LCDUse the smallest positive number divisible by every denominator.
MultiplyEvery termApply the LCD to each term on both sides.
SimplifyCancel denominatorsReduce each product carefully.
FinishSolve & checkUse standard linear-equation steps and substitute if needed.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Denominator

The number below the fraction bar.

5/8 → denominator 8

Least Common Denominator

The least common multiple of all denominators in the equation.

3 and 4 → LCD 12

Fractional Coefficient

A fraction multiplying a variable.

(2/5)x

Equivalent Equation

An equation with the same solution set as the original.

x/4 = 3 ↔ x = 12
04 · CORE RULE

Multiply the entire equation by the LCD.

LCD × EVERY TERM → CANCEL → SOLVE
05 · HOW IT WORKS

A reliable four-step process

01
List the denominators.Ignore whole-number terms because they have denominator 1.
02
Find the LCD.Choose the smallest number all denominators divide into evenly.
03
Multiply every term by the LCD.Use parentheses mentally so no term is skipped.
04
Solve the cleaner equation.Distribute, combine like terms, isolate the variable, and check.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — ONE DENOMINATOR

Solve: x/5 + 3 = 7

  1. Multiply every term by 5.5(x/5) + 5(3) = 5(7)
  2. Simplify.x + 15 = 35
  3. Subtract 15.x = 20
Check20/5 + 3 = 4 + 3 = 7 ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — TWO DENOMINATORS

Solve: x/3 + 1/2 = 5/2

LCD = 66(x/3) + 6(1/2) = 6(5/2)2x + 3 = 152x = 12x = 6

Important: The 6 multiplies every term, not just the fractions containing x.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — FRACTIONAL COEFFICIENT

Solve: (3/4)x − 2 = 7

4[(3/4)x] − 4(2) = 4(7)3x − 8 = 283x = 36x = 12
Check(3/4)(12) − 2 = 9 − 2 = 7 ✓
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — PARENTHESES

Solve: (x + 2)/4 = (x − 1)/3

LCD = 123(x + 2) = 4(x − 1)3x + 6 = 4x − 410 = x

Watch the numerator: A fraction bar groups the entire numerator. After clearing denominators, preserve those parentheses before distributing.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — SAT STYLE

A tank is 3/8 full. After 15 liters are added, it is 3/4 full. What is the tank's total capacity x, in liters?

(3/8)x + 15 = (3/4)x8[(3/8)x + 15] = 8[(3/4)x]3x + 120 = 6x120 = 3xx = 40
SAT connection

Translate each fraction of the total as a fractional coefficient times x. Then clear denominators before solving.

11 · WHEN YOU DO NOT NEED THE LCD

Sometimes one inverse operation is faster.

If the equation is simply x/7 = 5, multiplying both sides by 7 is already the LCD method. But if several denominators appear, using the LCD immediately is usually cleaner than combining fractions first.

x/7 = 5→x = 35
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Multiplying only some terms by the LCD

The LCD must multiply every term on both sides of the equals sign.

Using a common denominator that does not clear every fraction

For denominators 4 and 6, use 12 rather than 8.

Losing parentheses around a numerator

In (x + 2)/4, the whole numerator x + 2 is divided by 4.

Canceling across addition

You may cancel factors, not terms separated by + or −. Clear denominators by multiplication instead.

13 · SAT STRATEGY

Clear fractions before doing anything complicated.

Fraction-heavy equations often look harder than they are. The LCD can turn them into familiar integer equations in one line, reducing sign and arithmetic errors.

x/4 + x/6 = 5→3x + 2x = 60 → x = 12

15 · LESSON RECAP

Fractions first, ordinary algebra second.

  1. Identify every denominator.
  2. Find the least common denominator.
  3. Multiply every term by the LCD.
  4. Cancel denominators carefully.
  5. Solve the resulting linear equation.
  6. Check the solution in the original equation when useful.
a/b + c/dLCD = lcm(b,d)LCD × every term
