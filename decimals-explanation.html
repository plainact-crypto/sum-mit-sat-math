Equations with Decimals
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Turn decimal equations into cleaner equations you can solve confidently.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to solve linear equations containing decimal coefficients and constants either directly or by multiplying every term by a power of 10 to remove the decimals first.

0.4x + 1.2 = 3.6→x = 6
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Decimals do not change the equation rules.

You can add, subtract, multiply, and divide decimals directly. For school and exam preparation, clearing the decimals first can make arithmetic faster and reduce mistakes. Multiply every term on both sides by 10, 100, 1000, or another suitable power of 10.

1 decimal place× 100.6 becomes 6.
2 decimal places× 1000.25 becomes 25.
3 decimal places× 10000.008 becomes 8.
AlwaysEvery termMultiply all terms on both sides by the same number.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Decimal Coefficient

A decimal number multiplying a variable.

1.5x

Power of 10

A number such as 10, 100, or 1000 used to shift decimal points.

0.37 × 100 = 37

Equivalent Equation

An equation with exactly the same solution set.

0.5x = 4 ↔ 5x = 40

Place Value

The position of a digit relative to the decimal point.

0.04 = 4 hundredths
04 · CORE RULE

Use one power of 10 that clears every decimal in the equation.

MULTIPLY EVERY TERM → CLEAR DECIMALS → SOLVE
05 · HOW IT WORKS

A reliable four-step process

01
Inspect the decimal places.Find the greatest number of decimal places appearing in any term.
02
Choose a power of 10.Use 10 for one decimal place, 100 for two, and so on.
03
Multiply every term.Apply the same multiplier to every term on both sides.
04
Solve and check.Use ordinary linear-equation steps, then substitute if useful.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Solve: 0.4x + 1.2 = 3.6

  1. Multiply every term by 10.4x + 12 = 36
  2. Subtract 12.4x = 24
  3. Divide by 4.x = 6
Check0.4(6) + 1.2 = 2.4 + 1.2 = 3.6 ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — TWO DECIMAL PLACES

Solve: 1.5x − 0.75 = 3.75

100(1.5x − 0.75) = 100(3.75)150x − 75 = 375150x = 450x = 3

Shortcut: Direct decimal arithmetic also works. Clearing decimals is a strategy, not a new algebra rule.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — VARIABLE ON BOTH SIDES

Solve: 0.06x + 1.8 = 0.30x

100(0.06x + 1.8) = 100(0.30x)6x + 180 = 30x180 = 24xx = 7.5
Check0.06(7.5) + 1.8 = 0.45 + 1.8 = 2.25, and 0.30(7.5) = 2.25 ✓
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — PARENTHESES

Solve: 2.4(x − 1.5) = 8.4

24(x − 1.5) = 842.4(x − 1.5) = 8.4 → x − 1.5 = 3.5x = 5

Choose the cleanest route: Dividing both sides by 2.4 first is simpler here. You do not have to clear decimals if another legal step is faster.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — EXAM STYLE

A ride costs a $2.75 base fee plus $1.60 per mile. The total charge is $14.75. How many miles m were traveled?

2.75 + 1.60m = 14.75275 + 160m = 1475160m = 1200m = 7.5
Exam connectionMoney and measurement problems often produce decimals. Translate the situation first, then choose whether direct arithmetic or clearing decimals gives the cleaner equation.
11 · WHEN TO KEEP THE DECIMALS

Do not create extra work.

If the decimal calculation is simple, solve directly. For example, 0.5x = 6 is immediately x = 12. Clearing decimals is most useful when several decimal terms make the equation hard to read or compute accurately.

0.5x = 6→x = 12

Faster or not?

For a short equation, clearing decimals by hand is usually faster. Desmos is most useful as a verification tool when several decimal terms increase arithmetic risk.

Math cross-check: Multiplying the equation by 10 gives 4x + 12 = 36, so 4x = 24 and x = 6. Substitution confirms 0.4(6) + 1.2 = 3.6.

12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Moving the decimal in only one term

Multiplying by a power of 10 must affect every term on both sides.

Using too small a multiplier

If 0.025 appears, multiplying by 10 does not clear the decimal. Use 1000.

Dropping a zero

0.30 and 0.3 are equal, but 0.03 is ten times smaller. Track place value carefully.

Rounding too early

Keep exact decimal values through the algebra whenever possible. Round only when the problem specifically requires it.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Pick the representation that minimizes arithmetic risk.

For hand-solving, turning decimals into integers can make the structure easier to see. In a calculator-active problem, direct decimal arithmetic may be just as fast. The algebraic relationship must stay equivalent either way.

0.25x + 1.5 = 3.5→25x + 150 = 350 → x = 8

15 · LESSON RECAP

Decimal equations are ordinary linear equations in a different form.

  1. Identify the decimal places in the equation.
  2. Decide whether direct decimal arithmetic is already simple.
  3. If useful, choose a power of 10 that clears every decimal.
  4. Multiply every term on both sides by that same number.
  5. Solve the resulting linear equation.
  6. Check the answer in the original equation.
