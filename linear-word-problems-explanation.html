Linear Equation Word Problems

01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Turn a real situation into one linear equation, then solve it.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to identify an unknown quantity, translate relationships in a word problem into a linear equation, solve that equation, and interpret the answer in the original context.

start + change = final→write → solve → interpret
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

The hard part is usually translation, not algebra.

SAT word problems often describe a simple linear relationship using prices, distances, rates, ages, totals, or repeated charges. Your job is to decide what the variable represents and express every relevant statement with arithmetic.

UnknownDefine x clearlyGive the variable a meaning and unit.
RelationshipTranslate the wordsConvert quantities and operations into an equation.
AlgebraSolve carefullyUse inverse operations and preserve equality.
ContextInterpret the resultAnswer the quantity the question actually asks for.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Variable

A symbol representing the unknown quantity.

x = number of tickets

Constant

A fixed value in the situation.

$12 entrance fee

Rate

A quantity measured per one unit of another quantity.

$4 per ticket

Linear Model

A relationship where the variable changes at a constant rate.

total = fixed fee + rate × amount
04 · CORE RULE

Define the unknown before you write the equation.

DEFINE → TRANSLATE → SOLVE → CHECK → INTERPRET

06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC TOTAL

Maya has $18. After earning the same amount x dollars on each of 4 days, she has $50. How much does she earn per day?

  1. Define the variable.Let x be dollars earned per day.
  2. Translate the total.18 + 4x = 50
  3. Subtract 18.4x = 32
  4. Divide by 4.x = 8
InterpretationMaya earns $8 per day. Check: 18 + 4(8) = 50 ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — FIXED FEE PLUS RATE

A bike rental costs a $6 fixed fee plus $9 per hour. The total charge is $42. How many hours was the bike rented?

6 + 9h = 429h = 36h = 4

Translation pattern: total cost = fixed fee + hourly rate × number of hours.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — CONSECUTIVE VALUES

Two consecutive integers have a sum of 41. What are the integers?

x + (x + 1) = 412x + 1 = 412x = 40x = 20
InterpretationThe integers are 20 and 21. Their sum is 41 ✓
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — DISTANCE AND RATE

A car travels at a constant speed of 55 miles per hour. The trip is 220 miles. How many hours does the trip take?

distance = rate × time220 = 55tt = 4

Units check: miles ÷ miles per hour = hours.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — SAT STYLE

A streaming service charges a monthly base fee of $15 plus $3 for each premium movie rented. One month, the bill was $42. How many premium movies were rented?

15 + 3m = 423m = 27m = 9
SAT connection

When a situation has a fixed starting amount and a constant amount added for every unit, the equation usually has the form total = fixed amount + rate × number of units.

11 · TRANSLATION PATTERNS

Recognize the structure before touching the calculator.

Fixed + repeateda + bx = totalBase fee plus cost per unit.
Repeated onlybx = totalEqual groups, wages, distance at a fixed rate.
Differencex − a = bAmount left after a decrease or deduction.
Comparisonx + k or x − kOne quantity is k more or less than another.
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Choosing the wrong unknown

If the question asks for hours, define the variable as hours unless another choice clearly simplifies the model.

Reversing “less than” language

“5 less than x” means x − 5, not 5 − x.

Forgetting the fixed amount

A base fee must be included separately from a per-unit charge.

Solving correctly but answering the wrong quantity

If x is one part of the situation, check whether the question asks for x or for a related total.

Ignoring units or realism

A negative number of tickets or a fractional person is usually a signal that the model or interpretation needs another look.

13 · SAT STRATEGY

Use the answer choices only after you understand the model.

On multiple-choice questions, plugging choices into the situation can be a useful check, but building the equation first is usually more reliable. Label the variable, keep units visible, and estimate whether the final value is reasonable.

fixed + rate × units→total

15 · LESSON RECAP

Word problems become manageable when every symbol has a meaning.

  1. Read what the question is asking.
  2. Define the unknown quantity and its unit.
  3. Translate the relationship into one linear equation.
  4. Solve using valid algebraic steps.
  5. Substitute back when useful.
  6. Interpret the value in the original situation.
total = fixed + rate × unitsdistance = rate × timeconsecutive: x, x + 1
