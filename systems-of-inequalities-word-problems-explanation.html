Systems of Inequalities Word Problems
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Turn several real-world limits into one feasible region.

A system of inequalities models situations in which more than one condition must be true at the same time. The goal is to define the variables, translate each condition accurately, identify the overlap, and interpret only solutions that make sense in context.

02 · TRANSLATE THE LANGUAGE

Each phrase controls an inequality sign.

At most / no more than≤The quantity cannot exceed the stated limit.
At least / no fewer than≥The quantity must reach or exceed the stated minimum.
Less than<The boundary is not included.
More than>The boundary is not included.
03 · BUILD THE MODEL

Define variables and units before writing any inequality.

  1. State what x and y represent.
  2. Translate every independent limit or requirement.
  3. Add context restrictions such as x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0.
  4. If the quantities are counts, keep only whole-number solutions.
  5. Test or graph the overlap, then answer exactly what the question asks.
04 · MASTER EXAMPLE — EVENT KITS

A club prepares standard kits and premium kits.

Let x be the number of standard kits and y be the number of premium kits. The club can prepare at most 10 kits in total. A standard kit contributes 1 supply point and a premium kit contributes 2 supply points; the club needs at least 12 supply points.

x + y ≤ 10x + 2y ≥ 12x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0

For the boundary lines, write y = 10 - x and y = 6 - 0.5x. The feasible solutions in the first quadrant lie at or below the first boundary and at or above the second.

Boundary intersectionSet 10 − x = 6 − 0.5x. Then x = 8 and y = 2, so the boundaries meet at (8, 2).
05 · TEST A CANDIDATE

Would 4 standard kits and 5 premium kits work?

4 + 5 = 9 ≤ 10 ✓4 + 2(5) = 14 ≥ 12 ✓4 ≥ 0 and 5 ≥ 0 ✓

Because every condition is true, (4,5) is a feasible solution. A point must satisfy every inequality, not just one of them.

06 · REJECT A NON-SOLUTION

Would 2 standard kits and 3 premium kits work?

2 + 3 = 5 ≤ 10 ✓2 + 2(3) = 8 ≥ 12 ✗

The total-kit limit is satisfied, but the supply-point requirement is not. Therefore (2,3) is not a solution to the system.

07 · READ THE FEASIBLE REGION

The overlap is the set of choices that satisfy all conditions.

In the event-kit model, a valid point must be below or on x + y = 10, above or on x + 2y = 12, and in the first quadrant. If the variables count physical kits, valid answers must also use whole numbers.

ImportantA graph may contain decimal points in the overlap, but a word problem about counts can require integer coordinates only.
08 · MODELING CHECKLIST
CapacityUsually an upper bound“At most 10” becomes ≤ 10.
RequirementUsually a lower bound“At least 12” becomes ≥ 12.
Nonnegative quantitiesx ≥ 0, y ≥ 0Negative counts, lengths, or amounts usually do not fit the context.
Discrete quantitiesUse whole numbersPeople, tickets, kits, and many objects cannot be fractional.
09 · COMMON MISTAKES
Reversing “at most” and “at least”

At most means ≤. At least means ≥.

Forgetting a context restriction

A mathematically shaded region may include negative values even when the situation does not.

Checking only one inequality

A system solution must satisfy every condition simultaneously.

Reporting a decimal count

If variables count objects, use feasible whole-number points unless the context explicitly allows fractions.

10 · EXAM STRATEGY

Translate first. Solve second.

In an exam-style word problem, mark every phrase that signals a limit or minimum before doing algebra. Write the variables with units, create the inequalities, add nonnegativity or whole-number restrictions, and then use substitution, a graph, or Desmos to verify the feasible choices.

12 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Define variables and units.
  2. Translate every condition with the correct inequality sign.
  3. Add realistic restrictions such as nonnegativity and whole-number counts.
  4. Find the overlap that satisfies all inequalities.
  5. Verify candidate points and interpret the answer in context.
