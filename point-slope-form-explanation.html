Point-Slope Form
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Build a line from one point and a slope.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to recognize point-slope form, write the equation of a line when a slope and one point are known, and convert the result to other linear forms when needed.

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)→slope + one point
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Point-slope form stores a known point directly in the equation.

The form y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) uses a known point (x₁, y₁) and slope m. The signs inside the parentheses come from subtraction, so a negative coordinate can create a plus sign.

Formy − y₁ = m(x − x₁)Equation of a line through one known point.
SlopemRate of change of the line.
Known point(x₁, y₁)Any point that lies on the line.
Sign checkx − (−a) = x + aNegative coordinates change the visible sign.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Point-Slope Form

A linear equation form designed for a known slope and one known point.

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Known Point

A coordinate pair that satisfies the line.

(x₁, y₁)

Slope

The change in y divided by the change in x.

m = Δy / Δx
04 · CORE RULE

Substitute the slope and the coordinates exactly as signed values.

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
05 · HOW IT WORKS

Write point-slope form in four moves

01
Identify the slope.Set m equal to the given rate of change.
02
Identify the point.Read x₁ and y₁ from (x₁, y₁).
03
Substitute without changing signs early.Write y − (y₁) = m[x − (x₁)].
04
Simplify signs.For example, x − (−4) becomes x + 4.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Write the equation of a line with slope 3 through (2, 5).

  1. Identify the values.m = 3, x₁ = 2, y₁ = 5.
  2. Substitute.y − 5 = 3(x − 2)
  3. Check the point.Using x = 2 gives y − 5 = 0, so y = 5.
Answery − 5 = 3(x − 2) ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE COORDINATE

Write point-slope form for slope −2 through (−4, 7).

y − 7 = −2[x − (−4)]y − 7 = −2(x + 4)

Sign alert: Because x₁ = −4, subtracting x₁ creates x + 4.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — NEGATIVE Y-COORDINATE

Write point-slope form for slope 1/2 through (6, −3).

y − (−3) = (1/2)(x − 6)y + 3 = (1/2)(x − 6)
Checkx = 6 gives y + 3 = 0, so y = −3 ✓
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — CONVERT TO SLOPE-INTERCEPT FORM

Convert y − 4 = 2(x − 3) to y = mx + b.

y − 4 = 2x − 6y = 2x − 2

The slope remains 2. Expanding and isolating y reveals the y-intercept, −2.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — SAT STYLE

A quantity changes at a constant rate of 5 units per hour. After 3 hours, its value is 22. Write a linear equation using point-slope form.

m = 5(x₁, y₁) = (3, 22)y − 22 = 5(x − 3)
SAT connection

When a problem gives a rate and one observed value, point-slope form is often the fastest setup because you do not need to find the y-intercept first.

11 · READING A POINT FROM THE FORM

The signs inside the form are opposites of the coordinates.

Equationy − 6 = 4(x − 2)Point (2, 6).
Equationy + 5 = 3(x − 1)Point (1, −5).
Equationy − 8 = −2(x + 7)Point (−7, 8).
Equationy + 4 = (1/3)(x + 9)Point (−9, −4).
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Copying coordinate signs directly

For point (−3, 4), write x − (−3) = x + 3, not x − 3.

Switching x₁ and y₁

The x-coordinate belongs with x; the y-coordinate belongs with y.

Using the reciprocal of the slope

Use the given slope exactly unless the problem asks about a perpendicular line.

Forcing slope-intercept form too early

If slope and one point are already given, point-slope form is usually the quickest first step.

13 · SAT STRATEGY

Use the form that matches the information you are given.

If you know a slope and y-intercept, use slope-intercept form. If you know a slope and one point, use point-slope form. Choosing the matching form reduces unnecessary algebra and sign errors.

m + one point→point-slope form

15 · LESSON RECAP

Point-slope form is the direct route from one point and a slope to a line.

  1. Use y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
  2. m is the slope.
  3. (x₁, y₁) is a known point on the line.
  4. Substitute signed coordinates before simplifying signs.
  5. A negative coordinate may create a plus sign.
  6. Convert to slope-intercept or standard form only when useful.
y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)m = slope(x₁, y₁) = known point
