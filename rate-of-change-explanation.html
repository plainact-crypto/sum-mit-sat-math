Rate of Change
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Measure how one quantity changes compared with another.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to calculate rate of change from points, tables, graphs, equations, and real situations, then interpret the sign and units correctly.

(2, 5) and (6, 17)→rate of change = 3
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Rate of change compares output change with input change.

For two points on a linear relationship, subtract the y-values and divide by the corresponding change in x. Keep the subtraction order consistent in the numerator and denominator.

Change in outputΔy = y₂ − y₁How much the output changes.
Change in inputΔx = x₂ − x₁How much the input changes.
RateΔy / ΔxOutput change per 1 input unit.
Linear caseconstant rateThe same rate between any two points.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Rate of Change

The ratio of the change in output to the change in input.

Δy / Δx

Input

The independent quantity, usually represented by x.

x

Output

The dependent quantity, usually represented by y or f(x).

y
04 · CORE RULE / FORMULA

Subtract in matching order, then divide.

rate of change = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)

If you reverse the order in the numerator, reverse it in the denominator too. Both consistent orders give the same result.

05 · HOW IT WORKS

Use the form that gives the information most directly.

01
Two pointsUse (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁).
02

03

04

06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — TWO POINTS

Find the rate of change between (2, 5) and (6, 17).

Δy = 17 − 5 = 12Δx = 6 − 2 = 4rate = 12 / 4 = 3
MeaningThe output increases by 3 for each increase of 1 in the input.
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE RATE

Find the rate of change between (−1, 8) and (3, 0).

Δy = 0 − 8 = −8Δx = 3 − (−1) = 4rate = −8 / 4 = −2

Interpretation: A negative rate means the output decreases as the input increases.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — TABLE

A table contains the points (1, 7), (4, 16), and (7, 25). Find the rate of change.

Δy = 16 − 7 = 9Δx = 4 − 1 = 3rate = 9 / 3 = 3
Check another interval(25 − 16)/(7 − 4) = 9/3 = 3, so the rate is constant.
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — EQUATION

Find the rate of change of y = −4x + 11.

y = mx + bm = −4rate of change = −4

The coefficient of x is the rate of change when the equation is written in slope-intercept form.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — FRACTIONAL RATE

Find the rate of change between (2, 9) and (8, 12).

Δy = 12 − 9 = 3Δx = 8 − 2 = 6rate = 3 / 6 = 1/2
MeaningThe output increases by 1/2 unit for each 1-unit increase in input.
11 · WORKED EXAMPLE 6 — EXAM STYLE

A tank contains 90 liters of water at 2 minutes and 66 liters at 8 minutes. If the amount changes linearly, what is the rate of change?

Δwater = 66 − 90 = −24 litersΔtime = 8 − 2 = 6 minutesrate = −24 / 6 = −4 liters per minute
Exam connectionThe negative sign shows the tank is losing 4 liters each minute.
VISUAL GRAPH

See a constant rate of change as the slope of a line.

For y = 3x + 2, every increase of 1 in x increases y by 3. The graph is generated directly from the equation, so the visual slope and the algebraic rate of change are the same quantity.

Math cross-checkSubstitution gives (0, 2), (1, 5), and (2, 8). The rate from (0, 2) to (2, 8) is (8 − 2)/(2 − 0) = 6/2 = 3, matching the coefficient of x.
DESMOS STRATEGY

Use Desmos to verify a rate of change visually.

Enter

y = 3x + 2

Look for

A line that rises 3 units whenever x increases by 1, including the points (0,2), (1,5), and (2,8).

Use it to answer

Confirm that the constant rate of change is 3.

Why it works

For a linear function, the constant rate of change is exactly the slope of the graph and the coefficient of x in slope-intercept form.

Faster or not?

For two clean points or a simple equation, calculating the rate by hand is faster. Desmos is useful for checking the result and connecting the rate to the graph.

Math cross-check(8 − 2)/(2 − 0) = 3, and y = 3x + 2 gives y = 2, 5, 8 at x = 0, 1, 2.
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Mixing subtraction orders

If the numerator is y₂ − y₁, the denominator must be x₂ − x₁.

Dividing input change by output change

Rate of change is output change divided by input change, not the reverse.

Dropping the sign

A negative rate carries important meaning: the output decreases as the input increases.

Leaving out units

In context, write output units per input unit, such as dollars per hour or liters per minute.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Write the changes before you divide.

When a problem gives two values in a table, graph, or word problem, explicitly write the output change and input change first. This prevents reversed ratios and makes units easier to track.

change in output÷change in input

15 · LESSON RECAP

Rate of change tells how fast the output changes.

  1. Find the change in output.
  2. Find the matching change in input.
  3. Divide Δy by Δx.
  4. Keep subtraction order consistent.
  5. Interpret the sign and include units when the problem has a context.
Δy = y₂ − y₁Δx = x₂ − x₁rate = Δy / Δxy = mx + b → rate = m
