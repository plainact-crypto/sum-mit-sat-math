Polynomial Division
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Divide polynomials accurately and interpret remainders.

Divide by monomials or multi-term polynomials, then verify the result by rebuilding the dividend.

(6x³ + 9x² − 3x) ÷ 3x→2x² + 3x − 1
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Division reverses multiplication.

For a monomial divisor, divide every term. For a polynomial divisor, repeatedly divide leading terms, multiply back, subtract, and continue.

03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Dividend

The polynomial being divided.

Divisor

The expression you divide by.

Quotient

The result of division.

Remainder

What is left when its degree is lower than the divisor's degree.

04 · CORE RULE

Dividend = Divisor · Quotient + Remainder

xᵐ ÷ xⁿ = xᵐ⁻ⁿP(x)=D(x)Q(x)+R(x)

09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — MISSING POWER

(x³ − 5x + 6) ÷ (x − 2)

Write the dividend as x³ + 0x² − 5x + 6 before dividing. The zero placeholder prevents terms from sliding into the wrong columns.

x³ + 0x² − 5x + 6 = (x−2)(x²+2x−1)+4Quotient = x²+2x−1, remainder = 4
10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — REMAINDER THEOREM

Remainder of x² + 2x + 5 divided by x + 1

x+1 = x−(−1)P(−1)=1−2+5=4Remainder = 4

For a linear divisor x−a, evaluating P(a) is often faster than long division when only the remainder is requested.

11 · COMMON MISTAKES
Dividing only the first term

With a monomial divisor, divide every term.

Skipping missing powers

Use zero placeholders in long division.

Sign errors during subtraction

Subtract the entire product, including every sign.

Stopping too early

The remainder must have lower degree than the divisor.

12 · EXAM STRATEGY

Choose the shortest valid method.

Use term-by-term division for monomial divisors, factoring when the factors are obvious, the Remainder Theorem when only a remainder is asked, and long division for a general polynomial divisor.

14 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Divide every term when the divisor is a monomial.
  2. For long division: divide, multiply, subtract, repeat.
  3. Use zero placeholders for missing powers.
  4. Stop when the remainder degree is smaller than the divisor degree.
  5. Verify with dividend = divisor · quotient + remainder.
