Polynomial Division
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Divide polynomials accurately and interpret any remainder.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to divide a polynomial by a monomial and use polynomial long division when the divisor has more than one term.

(6x³ + 9x² − 3x) ÷ 3x→2x² + 3x − 1
02 · CORE IDEA

Division reverses multiplication.

When dividing like variable bases, divide the coefficients and subtract exponents. For a multi-term divisor, repeatedly divide the leading terms, multiply back, subtract, and bring down the next term.

xᵐ ÷ xⁿ = xᵐ⁻ⁿdividend = divisor · quotient + remainder
03 · WORKED EXAMPLE — MONOMIAL DIVISOR

Divide: (12x³ − 8x² + 4x) ÷ 4x

12x³/4x − 8x²/4x + 4x/4x3x² − 2x + 1

Each term is divisible by 4x. The exponent check is x³÷x=x² and x²÷x=x.

04 · WORKED EXAMPLE — EXACT LONG DIVISION

Divide: (x² + 5x + 6) ÷ (x + 2)

x² ÷ x = xx(x + 2) = x² + 2x(x² + 5x + 6) − (x² + 2x) = 3x + 63x ÷ x = 33(x + 2) = 3x + 6Quotient: x + 3
Multiplication check(x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 5x + 6.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE — REMAINDER

Divide: (2x² + 7x + 4) ÷ (x + 2)

2x² ÷ x = 2x2x(x + 2) = 2x² + 4x3x + 4 remains3x ÷ x = 33(x + 2) = 3x + 6Remainder: −2Answer: 2x + 3 − 2/(x + 2)

The remainder has lower degree than the divisor, so the division is complete.

06 · COMMON MISTAKES
Dividing only the first term

With a monomial divisor, divide every term in the polynomial.

Forgetting missing powers

Write a zero-coefficient placeholder when a power is missing during long division.

Subtracting incorrectly

After multiplying the divisor by the new quotient term, subtract the entire product and track every sign.

Stopping too early

Continue until the remainder has degree lower than the divisor.

07 · EXAM STRATEGY

Use the multiplication identity as your final check.

Rebuild the dividend using divisor × quotient + remainder. If it does not match exactly, revisit the first long-division step where the subtraction changed the expression.

09 · RECAP
  1. Divide coefficients and subtract exponents for monomial division.
  2. For long division, divide leading terms first.
  3. Multiply the divisor back and subtract carefully.
  4. Continue until the remainder degree is smaller than the divisor degree.
  5. Verify with dividend = divisor · quotient + remainder.
