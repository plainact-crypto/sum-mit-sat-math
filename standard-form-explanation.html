01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Recognize and use the standard form of a linear equation.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to identify equations written in standard form, name the coefficients and constant, and check whether an ordered pair satisfies a standard-form equation.

Ax + By = C→ example →3x + 2y = 12
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Standard form keeps the variable terms together.

A linear equation in two variables can be written as Ax + By = C, where A, B, and C are constants and A and B are not both zero. The x-term and y-term appear on one side, while the constant appears on the other.

x coefficientAThe number multiplying x.
y coefficientBThe number multiplying y.
ConstantCThe number on the other side.
LinearPower 1x and y are not squared, multiplied together, or in denominators.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Standard Form

A common form for a linear equation in two variables.

Ax + By = C

Coefficient

A number multiplying a variable.

5x − 3y = 9 → A = 5, B = −3

Constant

A number without a variable.

5x − 3y = 9 → C = 9

Solution

An ordered pair that makes the equation true.

(x, y)
04 · CORE RULE

Match the equation to Ax + By = C.

VARIABLE TERMS = CONSTANT
05 · HOW IT WORKS

Read standard form in three moves

01
Find the x-term.Its coefficient is A.
02
Find the y-term.Its coefficient is B, including its sign.
03
Find the constant side.That value is C.
04
Check linearity.The variables must appear only to the first power and cannot be multiplied together.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Identify A, B, and C in 4x + 7y = 20.

  1. Compare with Ax + By = C.4x + 7y = 20
  2. Read each coefficient and constant.A = 4, B = 7, C = 20.
AnswerA = 4, B = 7, C = 20 ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE COEFFICIENT

Identify A, B, and C in 6x − 5y = 18.

6x + (−5)y = 18A = 6B = −5C = 18

Watch the sign: The coefficient of y is −5, not 5.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — MISSING VARIABLE TERM

Is 3x = 15 a linear equation in two variables written in standard-form structure?

3x + 0y = 15A = 3, B = 0, C = 15

Yes. A or B may be zero; they simply cannot both be zero. Writing the zero term makes the structure visible.

09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — CHECK A SOLUTION

Does (2, 3) satisfy 3x + 2y = 12?

3(2) + 2(3) = 126 + 6 = 1212 = 12 ✓
ConclusionYes, (2, 3) is a solution.
10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — SAT STYLE

A ticket booth sells adult tickets for $12 and student tickets for $8. If x is the number of adult tickets and y is the number of student tickets, which equation represents $240 in total sales?

adult revenue = 12xstudent revenue = 8y12x + 8y = 240
SAT connection

Standard form is useful when two quantities contribute to one fixed total. Translate each contribution as coefficient × quantity, then add them to equal the total.

11 · WHAT COUNTS AS LINEAR?

Standard form describes a straight-line relationship.

Expressions such as x², xy, 1/x, or √y make an equation nonlinear. In a standard-form linear equation, x and y appear only to the first power.

Linear2x + 3y = 7Both variables have power 1.
Not linearx² + y = 7x is squared.
Not linearxy = 10The variables are multiplied.
Not linear1/x + y = 4x is in a denominator.
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Dropping a negative sign

In 4x − 3y = 9, B = −3.

Confusing a coefficient with a constant

Coefficients multiply variables; C is the standalone value on the constant side.

Thinking both coefficients must be positive

Standard form can contain negative coefficients. A common classroom convention may prefer A positive, but the equation remains mathematically equivalent either way.

Calling a nonlinear equation standard-form linear

Check for squares, products of variables, roots, or variables in denominators.

13 · SAT STRATEGY

When two categories add to a fixed total, look for standard form.

Cost, mixture, inventory, and total-value situations often translate naturally to Ax + By = C. Label what x and y count, attach the correct rate to each, and make the sum equal the stated total.

rate₁·x + rate₂·y=total

15 · LESSON RECAP

Standard form makes the two-variable structure easy to see.

  1. Use the pattern Ax + By = C.
  2. A and B are the coefficients of x and y.
  3. C is the constant on the other side.
  4. A and B cannot both be zero.
  5. Keep the signs attached to their coefficients.
  6. Check an ordered pair by substituting both coordinates into the equation.
Ax + By = C4x − 3y = 9 → A=4, B=−3, C=9
