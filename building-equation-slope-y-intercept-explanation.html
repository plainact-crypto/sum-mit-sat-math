Building an Equation from Slope and Y-Intercept
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Write a linear equation when the slope and y-intercept are known.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to place a given slope and y-intercept into slope-intercept form, interpret both values correctly, and check that the resulting equation matches the information given.

m = 3, b = −4→y = 3x − 4
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Slope-intercept form is built for this situation.

A linear function can be written as y = mx + b, where m is the slope and b is the y-intercept. If both values are already known, building the equation is mainly a matter of careful substitution and sign handling.

SlopemRate of change.
Y-interceptbValue of y when x = 0.
Formy = mx + bStandard setup for a linear function.
Checkx = 0The equation should give y = b.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Slope

The change in y for each 1-unit increase in x.

m = Δy / Δx

Y-Intercept

The point where the graph crosses the y-axis.

(0, b)

Slope-Intercept Form

A linear equation showing slope and y-intercept directly.

y = mx + b
04 · CORE RULE / FORMULA

Substitute the slope for m and the y-intercept for b.

y = mx + b

If the y-intercept is negative, keep the negative sign. If the slope is a fraction, keep the fraction exact unless a decimal is specifically required.

05 · HOW IT WORKS

Use a four-step process.

01

02

03

06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Write the equation of a line with slope 4 and y-intercept 7.

y = mx + bm = 4, b = 7y = 4x + 7
CheckAt x = 0, y = 7, so the y-intercept is correct.
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE INTERCEPT

Write the equation of a line with slope 2 and y-intercept −5.

y = mx + by = 2x + (−5)y = 2x − 5

Sign check: A y-intercept of −5 means the graph crosses the y-axis at (0, −5).

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — NEGATIVE SLOPE

Write the equation when m = −3 and b = 8.

y = mx + by = (−3)x + 8y = −3x + 8
MeaningFor every 1-unit increase in x, y decreases by 3.
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — FRACTIONAL SLOPE

A line has slope 3/5 and y-intercept −2. Write its equation.

m = 3/5b = −2y = (3/5)x − 2

Keeping the slope as 3/5 preserves the exact rate of change.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — FROM A CONTEXT

A tank contains 120 liters of water initially and gains 8 liters each minute. Write a linear equation for the amount y after x minutes.

initial value = 120 → b = 120rate of change = 8 → m = 8y = 8x + 120
CheckAt x = 0, the equation gives y = 120 liters, matching the initial amount.
11 · WORKED EXAMPLE 6 — EXAM STYLE

A linear function has y-intercept 15 and decreases by 2 units in y whenever x increases by 1. Which equation represents the function?

decreases by 2 → m = −2y-intercept 15 → b = 15y = −2x + 15
Exam connectionTranslate direction words such as “increases” and “decreases” into the sign of the slope before substituting.
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Switching m and b

The slope multiplies x. The y-intercept is the constant term.

Losing a negative sign

If b = −6, the equation ends with −6, not +6.

Using the intercept point as the slope

The y-intercept may be written as (0, b), but the slope is a separate rate of change.

Changing an exact fraction unnecessarily

If m = 2/3, writing y = (2/3)x + b is exact and usually preferable.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Identify m and b before writing the equation.

When information is presented in words, label the rate of change as m and the initial value as b. Then substitute once. This reduces sign mistakes and prevents mixing up the two quantities.

rate → m, initial value → b→y = mx + b

15 · LESSON RECAP

Known slope plus known y-intercept gives a direct equation.

  1. Start with y = mx + b.
  2. Substitute the slope for m.
  3. Substitute the y-intercept for b.
  4. Simplify the signs carefully.
  5. Check by setting x = 0 and confirming y = b.
