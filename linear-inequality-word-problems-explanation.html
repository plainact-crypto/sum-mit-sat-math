Linear Inequality Word Problems
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Translate limits, minimums, and maximums into linear inequalities.

Word problems often describe a range of allowed values instead of one exact answer. Your job is to define the variable, build the inequality, solve it, and interpret the solution in the real situation.

fixed amount + rate × quantity→compare with a limit or goal
02 · TRANSLATION WORDS

Decide what kind of boundary the wording requires.

At most · no more than≤The boundary is allowed.
Less than · below<The boundary is not allowed.
At least · no less than≥The boundary is allowed.
More than · greater than>The boundary is not allowed.
03 · MODELING ROUTINE

Build the inequality before solving.

  1. Define the variable.State exactly what the unknown counts or measures.
  2. Write the changing quantity.Use rate × variable, then include any fixed starting amount.
  3. Choose the comparison.Translate the limit or goal into <, ≤, >, or ≥.
  4. Solve and interpret.Use the context to decide whether the answer must be a whole number and whether to round up or down.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — MAXIMUM WITH A BUDGET

A ride costs $5 plus $2.50 per mile. You can spend no more than $30. What is the greatest number of miles?

5 + 2.5m ≤ 302.5m ≤ 25m ≤ 10
CheckAt 10 miles, the cost is 5+2.5(10)=30, so 10 is allowed. At 11 miles, the cost is 32.50, which is over budget.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — MINIMUM TO REACH A GOAL

You have saved $75 toward a $240 goal and add $15 each week. How many whole weeks are needed?

75 + 15w ≥ 24015w ≥ 165w ≥ 11
InterpretationAfter 10 weeks the total is $225, which is short. After 11 weeks the total is $240, so the least whole number is 11.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — DISCRETE ANSWER

A room holds at most 120 people. There are 34 people seated, and each new row seats 8. What is the greatest whole number of rows that can be added?

34 + 8r ≤ 1208r ≤ 86r ≤ 10.75

Context matters: A row count must be a whole number, so the greatest possible value is 10 rows, not 10.75 and not 11.

Check10 rows give 34+80=114 people. 11 rows give 34+88=122 people, which exceeds capacity.
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — STRICT BOUNDARY

A service costs $12 plus $1.50 per rental. The charge must stay below $30. What values of r are allowed?

12 + 1.5r < 301.5r < 18r < 12

Boundary: Exactly 12 rentals would cost $30, and “below $30” does not include $30. If rentals must be whole numbers, the greatest allowed count is 11.

08 · ROUNDING WITHOUT GUESSING

Let the inequality direction and the question decide what to do.

Greatest whole numberStay inside the allowed setIf x≤10.75, use 10.
Least whole numberReach the required setIf x≥10.2, use 11.
Continuous quantityKeep the exact boundaryDistance or time may allow decimals unless the context says otherwise.
Always verifySubstitute backCheck the chosen value and the nearest excluded value.
09 · DESMOS STRATEGY

Use Desmos as a fast model-and-boundary check.

Enter
For the budget example, enter 5+2.5m≤30. For the savings example, enter 75+15w≥240.

Look for
The solution interval Desmos highlights and the boundary value where the two sides are equal: m=10 in the first example and w=11 in the second.

Use it to answer
Match the highlighted solution set to the wording, then apply any whole-number restriction from the context.

Why it works
An inequality asks where one expression is less than or greater than another. The boundary occurs where the expressions are equal; the allowed side of that boundary is the solution set.

Faster or not?
For a short one- or two-step inequality, algebra is usually faster. Desmos is most useful for checking the model, confirming the boundary, or catching a wrong inequality direction.

Math cross-check5+2.5(10)=30 and 75+15(11)=240, so both claimed Desmos boundaries are exact. The nearby values 11 miles and 10 weeks fail their respective requirements.
10 · COMMON MISTAKES
Choosing the sign from a keyword alone

Read the full sentence. “At least $100 remains” describes the remaining amount, not necessarily the variable itself.

Ignoring the fixed amount

A setup fee, starting balance, existing pages, or fixed cost belongs in the model.

Rounding the wrong way

A maximum must stay under the limit; a minimum must actually reach the goal.

Answering with the variable but not the request

If the question asks for the greatest whole number, give that whole number and its unit.

11 · EXAM STRATEGY

Use the boundary as your fastest error check.

After solving, test the boundary in the original story. Then test the nearest value that should fail. This catches wrong signs, dropped fixed amounts, and incorrect rounding quickly.

13 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Define the unknown before writing the inequality.
  2. Include fixed amounts and per-unit rates.
  3. Translate the limit or goal with the correct comparison symbol.
  4. Solve the inequality, then interpret the solution in context.
  5. For discrete quantities, choose the greatest or least whole value that still satisfies the original condition.
  6. Substitute back to verify the boundary and units.
