Overlapping Solution Regions

Solutions correspond exactly to Practice Questions 1–18.

01 · SKILL CHECK

1. Point in the overlap

Answer: A) (2, 4)
At (2,4), 4 ≥ 2+1 and 4 ≤ −2+7. Both inequalities are true, so the point is in the overlap.

2. Describe the overlapping region

Answer: A) Above y = 2 and left of or on x = 3
y>2 means strictly above the horizontal boundary y=2. x≤3 means left of the vertical boundary x=3, including that boundary.

3. Intersection of the boundary lines

Answer: B) (2, 4)
Set x+2=−2x+8. Then 3x=6, so x=2 and y=4. Substitution gives 4=2+2 and 4=−4+8.
02 · CORE PRACTICE

4. Point satisfying both inequalities

Answer: A) (2, 3)
For (2,3), 3≤2(2)+1=5 and 3≥−2+4=2. Both conditions hold.

5. Region between parallel lines

Answer: A) The strip between the two parallel lines, including both boundaries
The conditions x−2≤y≤x+3 place y on or above the lower parallel line and on or below the upper parallel line. Both inequalities include equality.

6. x-values where overlap exists

Answer: C) x > 2
A y-value can be both above −x+5 and below x+1 only when −x+5<x+1. Solving gives 4<2x, so x>2.

7. Point satisfying three constraints

Answer: A) (0, 2)
0≥−1, 2≤3, and 0+2=2≥1. The other choices violate at least one constraint.

8. Intersection of y = 3x − 4 and y = x + 2

Answer: B) (3, 5)
Set 3x−4=x+2. Then 2x=6, so x=3 and y=5. Both equations give 5.

9. Greatest possible x-value

Answer: B) 3
In the first quadrant, y≥0 and y≤−2x+6 require −2x+6≥0. Thus x≤3, and x=3 is attainable at y=0.

10. System containing (1, 4)

Answer: A) y ≥ 2x + 1 and y ≤ −x + 6
At x=1, the bounds are y≥3 and y≤5. Since y=4 lies between them, (1,4) satisfies both.

11. Intersection of x + y = 7 and x − y = 1

Answer: B) (4, 3)
Add the equations to get 2x=8, so x=4. Then y=3. Check: 4+3=7 and 4−3=1.
03 · EXAM-STYLE PRACTICE

12. Feasible workshop production pair

Answer: A) (4, 1)
For (4,1), 4x+2y=18≤24 and x+y=5≥5, with both values nonnegative. Each other option violates at least one constraint.

13. x-values that can produce overlap

Answer: A) x ≤ 4
Overlap requires 0.5x+2≤−x+8. Then 1.5x≤6, so x≤4. At x=4 the two boundaries meet at y=4, so equality is allowed.

14. Ticket order satisfying both limits

Answer: A) (4, 5)
The total is 9≤10 and revenue is 12(4)+6(5)=78≤84. The other listed orders exceed the ticket limit, the revenue limit, or both.

15. Greatest possible x-value in the feasible region

Answer: C) 4
For the lower and upper y-bounds to overlap, x−1≤7−x. Thus 2x≤8 and x≤4. The condition x≥2 permits x=4, where y=3.

16. Parallel strict inequalities

Answer: A) The solution regions do not overlap
The first inequality requires y above −3x+2, while the second requires y below −3x−1. Since −3x+2 is always 3 units higher, no y can satisfy both.
04 · CHALLENGE PROBLEMS

17. Greatest integer x-value with a solution

Answer: C) 3
The upper bound is y≤−x+8. For compatibility with y≥2x−1, require 2x−1≤−x+8, giving 3x≤9 and x≤3. The other lower bound x+y≥7 means y≥7−x, which is compatible with y≤8−x. At x=3, y=5 satisfies all three constraints, so 3 is attainable.

18. Greatest possible k

Answer: C) 2
At x=2, the system becomes y≥2+k and y≤4. At least one y exists only if 2+k≤4, so k≤2. Therefore the greatest possible value is 2.
