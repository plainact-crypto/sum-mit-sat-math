Converting Between Forms

01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Rewrite the same line in different useful forms.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to convert a linear equation among standard form, slope-intercept form, and point-slope form without changing the line it represents.

2x + y = 7→y = −2x + 7
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Different forms show different information.

Equivalent linear equations can look different while representing exactly the same set of points. Choose the form that makes the needed information easiest to see.

Standard formAx + By = CUseful for fixed totals and intercepts.
Slope-intercepty = mx + bShows slope m and y-intercept b immediately.
Point-slopey − y₁ = m(x − x₁)Useful when slope and one point are known.
Key ideaEquivalentAlgebra changes the form, not the line.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Equivalent Equations

Equations with the same solution set.

2x + y = 7 ↔ y = −2x + 7

Slope

The rate of change of a line.

m

Y-Intercept

The y-value when x = 0.

b

Ordered Pair

A point written as (x, y).

(x₁, y₁)
04 · CORE RULE

Use legal algebraic operations to isolate or regroup terms.

CHANGE THE FORM — KEEP THE LINE
05 · HOW IT WORKS

Pick the target form first

01
To slope-intercept form:Isolate y so the equation reads y = mx + b.
02
To standard form:Move x- and y-terms to one side and the constant to the other.
03
To point-slope form:Identify m and a point (x₁, y₁), then substitute into y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
04
Verify equivalence:Reverse the algebra or test a point that lies on the line.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — STANDARD TO SLOPE-INTERCEPT

Rewrite 3x + 2y = 14 in slope-intercept form.

  1. Move the x-term.2y = −3x + 14
  2. Divide every term by 2.y = −(3/2)x + 7
Answery = −(3/2)x + 7 ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — SLOPE-INTERCEPT TO STANDARD

Rewrite y = 4x − 9 in standard form.

y = 4x − 9−4x + y = −94x − y = 9

Equivalent forms: Both −4x + y = −9 and 4x − y = 9 describe the same line. Multiplying an entire equation by −1 does not change its solutions.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — POINT-SLOPE TO SLOPE-INTERCEPT

Rewrite y − 5 = 2(x − 3) in slope-intercept form.

y − 5 = 2x − 6y = 2x − 1
Check the pointAt x = 3, y = 2(3) − 1 = 5 ✓
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — STANDARD TO POINT-SLOPE

Write 2x + y = 8 in point-slope form using the point (2, 4).

y = −2x + 8m = −2y − 4 = −2(x − 2)
Point check2(2) + 4 = 8 ✓
10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — EXAM STYLE

A line is given by 6x + 3y = 24. What is its slope?

3y = −6x + 24y = −2x + 8m = −2
Exam connection

If a question asks for slope and the equation is not already in y = mx + b form, isolate y. The coefficient of x is then the slope.

VISUAL GRAPH

Equivalent forms produce the same line.

Compare 2x + y = 7 with its slope-intercept form y = −2x + 7. They describe the same points, so their graphs coincide exactly.

Math cross-checkFor x = 0, 1, 2, the equation y = −2x + 7 gives y = 7, 5, 3. Substitution into 2x + y = 7 gives 7 each time, confirming both forms contain the same verified points.
DESMOS STRATEGY

Use a graphing tool to verify that two forms are equivalent.

Enter

y = -2x + 7 and 2x + y = 7

Look for

The two graphs lying exactly on top of each other.

Use it to answer

If the graphs coincide, the equations represent the same line and the conversion preserved the solution set.

Why it works

Equivalent equations are true for exactly the same ordered pairs, so they generate the same graph.

Faster or not?

Manual algebra is faster for converting the form; Desmos is useful as a final equivalence check.

Math cross-checkRearranging 2x + y = 7 gives y = −2x + 7. At x = 0, 1, 2 both forms give (0, 7), (1, 5), and (2, 3).
11 · WHY CONVERT?

The best form depends on the question.

Need slope?y = mx + bRead m directly.
Need y-intercept?y = mx + bRead b directly.
Know slope + point?Point-slopeSubstitute immediately.
Two quantities total?StandardAx + By = C often matches the context naturally.
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Dividing only one term

When solving for y, divide every term on both sides by the coefficient of y.

Moving a term without changing its sign

Subtracting 3x from both sides turns +3x into −3x on the other side.

Forgetting to distribute in point-slope form

For y − 5 = 2(x − 3), distribute 2 to both x and −3.

Changing only part of an equation by −1

If you multiply by −1, multiply every term on both sides.

13 · EXAM STRATEGY

Convert only as far as the question requires.

If a test-style question asks only for slope, stop once y is isolated enough to read the coefficient of x. If it asks for a standard-form equation, move terms and simplify; do not do extra algebra that creates more chances for sign errors.

15 · LESSON RECAP

One line can wear several algebraic forms.

  1. Standard form is Ax + By = C.
  2. Slope-intercept form is y = mx + b.
  3. Point-slope form is y − y₁ = m(x − x₁).
  4. Use inverse operations and distribution to convert between forms.
  5. Apply operations to the entire equation consistently.
  6. Equivalent forms represent the same points and the same line.
Ax + By = Cy = mx + by − y₁ = m(x − x₁)
