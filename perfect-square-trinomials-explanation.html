Perfect-Square Trinomials
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Recognize and factor perfect-square trinomials quickly.

A perfect-square trinomial is the result of squaring a binomial. Recognizing its pattern lets you factor it without searching through factor pairs.

x² + 10x + 25→(x + 5)²
02 · CORE PATTERNS

Square the first term, square the last term, and check the middle term.

a² + 2ab + b² = (a+b)²a² − 2ab + b² = (a−b)²

The first and last terms must be perfect squares, and the middle term must equal ±2ab.

03 · RECOGNITION CHECK
  1. Find the square root of the first term: a.
  2. Find the square root of the last term: b.
  3. Calculate 2ab.
  4. If the middle term is +2ab, use (a+b)².
  5. If the middle term is −2ab, use (a−b)².
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — POSITIVE MIDDLE TERM

Factor: x² + 12x + 36

√(x²)=x and √36=62(x)(6)=12x ✓x²+12x+36=(x+6)²
Math cross-check(x+6)²=x²+12x+36.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — NEGATIVE MIDDLE TERM

Factor: y² − 14y + 49

√(y²)=y and √49=7−2(y)(7)=−14y ✓y²−14y+49=(y−7)²
Math cross-check(y−7)²=y²−14y+49.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — COEFFICIENT ON x²

Factor: 9x² + 24x + 16

√(9x²)=3x and √16=42(3x)(4)=24x ✓9x²+24x+16=(3x+4)²
Math cross-check(3x+4)²=9x²+24x+16.
07 · NOT EVERY TWO SQUARES MAKE THE PATTERN

Is x² + 8x + 25 a perfect-square trinomial?

The outside terms are squares, but that is not enough.

√(x²)=x and √25=52(x)(5)=10x8x ≠ 10x

Conclusion: it is not a perfect-square trinomial. Always verify the middle term.

08 · GCF FIRST

Factor completely: 2x² + 20x + 50

2(x²+10x+25)=2(x+5)²

A common factor can hide the perfect-square pattern. Check for a GCF before testing the trinomial.

09 · WHY THE PATTERN WORKS

Multiplying a binomial by itself creates two identical cross-products.

(a+b)(a+b)=a²+ab+ab+b²=a²+2ab+b²

The minus pattern works the same way: (a−b)²=a²−2ab+b².

10 · COMMON MISTAKES
Checking only the first and last terms

You must also verify that the middle term is exactly ±2ab.

Using the sign of the constant

In both patterns the final square term is positive. The middle term determines the sign inside the binomial.

Forgetting the coefficient square root

For 9x², the first binomial term is 3x, not 9x.

Missing a GCF

Factor the GCF first, then inspect what remains.

11 · EXAM STRATEGY

Use the middle-term test before doing longer factoring.

When the first and last terms are squares, compute twice their square-root product. If it matches the middle term, the factorization is immediate. In multiple-choice work, expanding a proposed squared binomial is also a fast check.

13 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Perfect-square trinomials come from squaring binomials.
  2. Both outside terms must be perfect squares.
  3. The middle term must equal ±2ab.
  4. The middle-term sign determines + or − inside the squared binomial.
  5. Factor out any GCF first.
  6. Expand the result to verify it.
