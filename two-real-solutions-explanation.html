Two Real Solutions
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Recognize when a quadratic has two distinct real solutions.

A quadratic has two real solutions when the quadratic formula produces two different real values.

02 · CORE RULE

Two distinct real solutions mean the discriminant is positive.

D=b²−4acD>0 ⇔ two distinct real solutions

When D>0, √D is a positive real number. The plus and minus branches of the quadratic formula therefore give two different real roots.

03 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1

Solve x²−5x+6=0.

D=(−5)²−4(1)(6)=25−24=1>0x=(5±1)/2x=2 or x=3
Math cross-checkx²−5x+6=(x−2)(x−3). Substitution gives 4−10+6=0 and 9−15+6=0.
04 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2

Find c so x²−8x+c=0 has two distinct real solutions.

D=64−4c64−4c>0c<16

The strict inequality matters: c=16 gives one repeated root, not two distinct roots.

05 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3

For what k does 2x²+kx+12=0 have two real solutions?

D=k²−4(2)(12)=k²−96k²−96>0|k|>4√6k<−4√6 or k>4√6
Boundary checkAt k=±4√6, D=0, so the boundary values must be excluded.

06 · COMMON MISTAKES
Using D≥0

D≥0 includes the repeated-root case. Two distinct real solutions require D>0.

Solving k²>A as one-sided

For positive A, k²>A means k<−√A or k>√A.

Forgetting the equation must stay quadratic

If a parameter is the coefficient of x², exclude any value that makes that coefficient zero.

07 · EXAM STRATEGY

Translate “two distinct real solutions” into D>0 immediately.

Write the equation in standard form, identify a, b, and c, then solve b²−4ac>0. Check boundary values and any value that would make a=0.

Show quick-check answers

09 · LESSON RECAP
  1. Write the quadratic in standard form.
  2. Two distinct real solutions mean D>0.
  3. Solve the resulting inequality carefully.
  4. Exclude equality because D=0 gives only one real solution.
  5. Verify roots by substitution when exact values are available.
