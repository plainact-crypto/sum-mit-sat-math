Polynomial Multiplication
Advanced Math
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Multiply polynomials accurately by distributing every term.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to multiply a monomial by a polynomial and multiply two binomials, then combine like terms to write the product in standard form.

(x + 3)(x + 5)→x² + 8x + 15
02 · CORE IDEA

Every term in one factor must multiply every term in the other.

Polynomial multiplication is repeated use of the distributive property. Multiply coefficients, add exponents on matching variable bases, and then combine only like terms.

a(b + c) = ab + acxᵐ · xⁿ = xᵐ⁺ⁿ
03 · WORKED EXAMPLE — MONOMIAL

Multiply: 3x(2x² − 5x + 4)

3x·2x² − 3x·5x + 3x·46x³ − 15x² + 12x

Each term receives the factor 3x. For example, x·x² = x³ because the exponents add.

04 · WORKED EXAMPLE — TWO BINOMIALS

Multiply: (x + 4)(x − 2)

x(x − 2) + 4(x − 2)x² − 2x + 4x − 8x² + 2x − 8
Key checkThere are four pairwise products before like terms are combined.
05 · WORKED EXAMPLE — LEADING COEFFICIENTS

Multiply: (2x − 3)(x + 5)

2x² + 10x − 3x − 152x² + 7x − 15
06 · COMMON MISTAKES
Skipping a pair

Make sure every term in the first factor multiplies every term in the second.

Adding exponents across addition

The exponent rule applies during multiplication: x²·x³=x⁵. It does not mean x²+x³=x⁵.

Sign errors

Track negative factors before combining like terms.

Combining unlike terms

x² and x are not like terms and cannot be combined.

07 · EXAM STRATEGY

Use structure before speed.

For two binomials, write the four products explicitly when signs are mixed. Mental shortcuts are useful only when they do not hide a missed product.

09 · RECAP
  1. Distribute every term to every term.
  2. Multiply coefficients and add exponents for matching bases.
  3. Track signs carefully.
  4. Combine like terms.
  5. Write the result in descending powers.
