Algebra · Linear Equations in One Variable

Variables on Both Sides
Algebra
01 · LESSON OBJECTIVE

Bring the variable terms together.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to solve linear equations with variable terms on both sides by simplifying, moving all variable terms to one side, moving constants to the other side, and checking the result.

3x + 5 = x + 17→x = 6
02 · WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

Both sides may contain x, but the goal is still one x-side.

An equation remains balanced when you perform the same operation on both sides. When x appears on both sides, subtract one variable term from both sides so every variable term ends up on one side of the equation.

FirstSimplifyDistribute and combine like terms on each side.
ThenCollect xMove all variable terms to one side.
NextCollect constantsMove number-only terms to the other side.
FinallySolve & checkMake the coefficient 1, then substitute.
03 · KEY DEFINITIONS

Variable Term

A term containing the variable.

5x, −2x, x

Constant

A term with no variable.

7, −4, 12

Identity

An equation true for every value of the variable.

4x + 7 = 4x + 7

Contradiction

An equation that becomes a false statement, so it has no solution.

3 = −5
04 · CORE RULE

Move variable terms to one side before the final solve.

SIMPLIFY → COLLECT x → COLLECT CONSTANTS → SOLVE
05 · HOW IT WORKS

A reliable four-step process

01
Simplify both sides.Distribute and combine like terms if needed.
02
Choose a variable side.Subtract one variable term from both sides. Choosing the larger positive coefficient often keeps the arithmetic cleaner.
03
Move the constants.Use inverse operations until only the variable term remains.
04
Finish and check.Divide by the coefficient, then substitute into the original equation.
06 · WORKED EXAMPLE 1 — BASIC

Solve: 3x + 5 = x + 17

  1. Move x-terms to one side.Subtract x from both sides.2x + 5 = 17
  2. Move the constant.Subtract 5 from both sides.2x = 12
  3. Divide by 2.x = 6
Check3(6) + 5 = 6 + 17 → 23 = 23 ✓
07 · WORKED EXAMPLE 2 — CHOOSE THE CLEANER SIDE

Solve: 7x − 4 = 3x + 20

4x − 4 = 204x = 24x = 6

Why subtract 3x? It leaves a positive 4x. Subtracting 7x would also work, but the arithmetic would involve a negative coefficient.

08 · WORKED EXAMPLE 3 — DISTRIBUTE FIRST

Solve: 2(2x + 3) = 3x + 11

4x + 6 = 3x + 11x + 6 = 11x = 5
Check2(2·5 + 3) = 3·5 + 11 → 26 = 26 ✓
09 · WORKED EXAMPLE 4 — SIGN CONTROL

Solve: 5 − 2x = 3x − 15

5 = 5x − 1520 = 5xx = 4

Key move: Adding 2x to both sides removes the negative variable term on the left and keeps the solving path clean.

10 · WORKED EXAMPLE 5 — SAT STYLE

Two ride plans have the same total cost. Plan A costs $12 plus $4 per ride. Plan B costs $2 plus $6 per ride. For how many rides are the costs equal?

12 + 4x = 2 + 6x10 + 4x = 6x10 = 2xx = 5
SAT connection

When two quantities are described as equal, write one expression for each quantity and set them equal. The variable naturally appears on both sides.

11 · SPECIAL CASES

Sometimes x disappears.

All real numbers

If both sides reduce to the same true statement, every real x works.

4x + 7 = 4x + 7 → 7 = 7

No solution

If the variable cancels and leaves a false statement, no value of x works.

3x + 2 = 3x − 5 → 2 = −5
12 · COMMON MISTAKES
Moving a term by changing its sign without an operation

Think in balanced operations: subtract 3x from both sides rather than saying “move 3x.”

Combining terms across the equals sign

You may combine like terms only on the same side until you perform a valid operation on both sides.

Skipping distribution

In 2(2x + 3), distribute first to get 4x + 6.

Assuming every equation has one numerical answer

If x cancels completely, check whether the remaining statement is always true or impossible.

13 · SAT STRATEGY

Choose the subtraction that keeps your coefficient positive.

Both directions are mathematically valid. On a timed test, cleaner signs usually mean fewer mistakes. If the coefficients are 7x and 3x, subtracting 3x is typically easier.

8x + 1 = 5x + 19→3x + 1 = 19 → x = 6

15 · LESSON RECAP

One equation, one variable side.

  1. Simplify each side first.
  2. Collect all variable terms on one side.
  3. Collect constants on the other side.
  4. Solve the resulting one-variable equation.
  5. If the variable cancels, identify all real numbers or no solution.
  6. Check a numerical solution in the original equation.
ax + b = cx + d(a − c)x = d − b
